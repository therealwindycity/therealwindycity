## Bottom line at this source cutoff

The [September 28 City Council agenda](https://cheyenne.granicus.com/GeneratedAgendaViewer.php?view_id=5&event_id=1445) scheduled three third-reading ordinances—annexation, assigned AG/P zoning, and AG-to-BP zoning—as items 6–8, and a separate PlanCheyenne/Future Land Use Map and Urban Service Boundary resolution as item 23. The agenda is not the action record. On the City's [minutes/archive page](https://www.cheyennecity.org/Your-Government/City-Council/Minutes-and-Agendas), the September 28 row displayed an agenda and video link but no linked minutes as of October 2. A [September 29 news article](https://capcity.news/news/2026/09/28/cheyenne-approves-1260-acre-cox-ranch-addition-after-final-vote/) reports that the package passed 8–2 and was effective immediately; that remains a **secondary report**, not official verification. The City-hosted agenda attachments expressly warn that support documents may be the versions first submitted and may not include later amendments.

The strongest open legal/record question is the actual boundary geometry and the basis for the annexation finding under Wyo. Stat. § 15-1-402(a)(iv). City staff reports roughly **5.26%** contiguity for the Cox Ranch land. *BJ Hough, LLC v. City of Cheyenne* affirmed a fact-specific finding based on contact, location, and character, with alternative reported measures of about **13%** and **6%–10%**; the Wyoming Supreme Court said it had never defined an exact percentage. Neither number is a statutory safe harbor or a numeric minimum. The final annexation map, perimeter method, parcel treatment, full council findings, and the filed petition are necessary before drawing any conclusion.

The reviewed documents also contain materially different acreage descriptions (below). Do not combine, normalize, or silently “correct” them without the executed ordinance and recorded map.

## Case-law comparison for the contiguity row

1. **Controlling standard:** *Board of County Commissioners of Laramie County v. City of Cheyenne*, 2004 WY 16, 85 P.3d 999, ¶ 31. The Court construed “contiguous with or adjacent to” to require physical touching to some substantial degree (not merely nearby land), while recognizing that the shared border need not be lengthy.
2. **Later application:** *BJ Hough, LLC v. City of Cheyenne*, 2012 WY 140, 287 P.3d 761, ¶¶ 123–27. The Swan Ranch record contained alternative measurements—about 13% at interstate rights-of-way or about 6%–10% at the northeast boundary. The Supreme Court said no exact percentage had been defined and affirmed on the combined “degree of contact, location, and character” under the case’s record and review posture.
3. **Limit:** Neither case decides the Cox Ranch boundary. The figures may use different parcel boundaries, denominators, right-of-way assumptions and physical contact points; the final City boundary/map and source GIS are essential. The cases do not supply a numeric threshold, and a staff percentage is not a judicial finding.
4. **How the cases cut both ways:** *Board of County Commissioners* is strong adverse authority against annexing land that does not physically touch or qualify for a statutory exception; the Court voided that annexation. But its facts involved no shared boundary, not a measured low percentage of actual contact. *BJ Hough* is potentially favorable to the City because it affirmed an annexation with comparatively low reported contact measurements and rejected a fixed-percentage test; it is also fact-specific and requires the contact’s degree, location, and character to be assessed. Which analogy matters depends on the final recorded Cox boundary and § 15-1-407 status.
5. **Source caution:** Full *BJ Hough* text and its citation were checked in a CourtListener reproduction. An official Wyoming Judicial Branch opinion scan was not located in this search. Verify the official opinion before any court-facing or final legal use.
