## Identity and scope notes

The City of Cheyenne's 2026 candidate packet lists **Lawrence J. Wolfe** and **Pete Laybourn**. Auto-captions in the archive often render these surnames as “Wolf,” “Leborn,” or similar; on-screen names below use the City's spelling. The evidence is spread across different projects and agenda items. The sequence below is a comparison of public statements and actions, not proof that either member changed a legal opinion or that Miller was legally vindicated. In particular:

- Wolfe's June 22, 2026 “no authority to disagree” statement is in a discussion about a **Swan Ranch business-park expansion** at Clear Creek Parkway/Gannett Peak Drive. It is **not** a Cox Ranch item.
- Laybourn's May 11 exchange is about **West Edge/Reed Avenue**, not Cox Ranch. His June 15 exchange is about a rezoning/planning issue, not the June 22 industrial-siting discussion.
- The August 28 work session is explicitly a Cox Ranch update, but the chair says there is no public input at that work session. Do not describe the developers' work-session presentation as a public-comment slot.
- The September 14 vote is a Cox Ranch second-reading vote. A vote against an ordinance is an action; it does not, by itself, establish agreement with Miller's legal theory.

### Legal interstitial (research lead, not a conclusion)

The June 22 discussion names the Wyoming Industrial Development Information and Siting Act. A 2025 statutory text reproduction of **Wyo. Stat. § 35-12-119(a)** says nonmineral-processing facilities to be built in existing industrial parks, as designated by local governments, are exempt from payment of fees and certification procedures, while specified information must still be furnished to the Division when the statutory facility criteria apply. Subsection (d) addresses jurisdiction over exempt activities and information filings. [2025 text reproduction of § 35-12-119](https://law.justia.com/codes/wyoming/title-35/chapter-12/section-35-12-119/) · [Wyoming Legislative Service Office, 2022 Industrial Siting topic summary](https://wyoleg.gov/InterimCommittee/2022/09-202204258-05TopicSummary-IndustrialSiting.pdf)

**Do not render that as “the statute proves Miller was right” or “the Council had no power.”** The source question is narrower: what facility is covered, whether the relevant facility/phase meets statutory criteria, whether the site qualifies as an existing locally designated industrial park, what information/fees/certification apply, and what the specific city action was. Before any public or court-facing cut, verify the then-effective official Wyoming code and the underlying designation/permit record. No case is cited in this first storyboard because the supplied archive has not yet established a controlling case holding for this fact pattern.
