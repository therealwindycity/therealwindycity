Dear Cheyenne,
Wyoming is overwhelmingly a Republican-voting state, with citizens who largely believe in the principles, platforms and resolutions of the Republican Party. Republican voters understand that honesty, integrity and fairness are essential to our ability to self govern.
Our constitutional representative republic remains one of the greatest political systems derived from the lessons of history and an understanding of human nature. It was designed to prevent tyranny, assure equality and opportunity, and protect our individual rights. However, its effectiveness depends upon the honesty and integrity of those elected to represent us.
When elected officials misrepresent their political beliefs to gain the confidence of voters, they undermine the very foundation of representative government.
The division within the Republican Party was on full display Tuesday evening, Nov. 17, at the Laramie County Republican Central Committee meeting.
The Laramie County Republican Party voted to pay its share of dues to the Wyoming Republican Party, a primary source of funding for our state party. Twenty elected precinct committeemen and committeewomen voted against paying those dues. Their names were recorded in a roll-call vote, making their positions a matter of record. Call them out.
Failure to remain current with our financial obligations to the state party could result in the entire Laramie County delegation being denied seating at the Republican State Convention in May 2027. Such an outcome would deprive Laramie County Republicans of their voice in shaping the direction of our party at the state level.
These committee members were elected to represent Republican voters. Yet their votes raise a serious question: Why would elected Republican representatives oppose financially supporting the very party under whose banner they serve?
This internal division contributes to the dysfunction of the Republican Party and undermines public confidence in our elected officials. Unless Wyoming voters become more engaged and demand accountability, little will change.
More than 15,000 Laramie County voters stayed home during this past election. We must ask ourselves why so many citizens have become disengaged from the political process. Is this because they believe the process is hopeless as identified in our recent meeting?
Steve Johnson,
Cheyenne
