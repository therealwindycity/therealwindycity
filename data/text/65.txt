Obituaries: Prevedel; Boyd; Gross
Frank Prevedel:
1932 – 2026
Frank Prevedel passed away on November 12, 2026. He was born in South Superior, Wyoming, on December 2, 1932, to Frank Prevedel and Ines Tarter Prevedel. He graduated from Superior High School in 1950 and from the University of Wyoming and the University of Denver.
Frank worked for Sweetwater School District #1 as a high school teacher and speech & debate coach and as Assistant Superintendent for Curriculum. Later, he founded the Sweetwater BOCES, serving as its first director. He was elected to the first board of Western Wyoming Community College (serving as Chairman) and to the Wyoming State Senate four times. He served on the WICHE Commission and as Chairman of the Blue Ribbon Wyoming Commission on Education.
He loved gardening, backpacking in the Wind Rivers, and spending time at his cabin in Atlantic City, Wyoming. Superior was close to his heart. He procured funding to restore the historic Union Hall and loved leading tour groups to town. He wrote Superior and South Superior, a book in the “Images of America” series.
Frank is survived by Norma, his wife of 63 years; children: Maria (Andy) Harwood, Michael (Michelle) Prevedel, Matthew (Cindy) Prevedel, Amy Prevedel (Stewart Slafter) and Anna (Jimmy) Tsiamis; grandchildren: Erin Harwood, August Prevedel, John (Sasha) Prevedel, Sara Jessen, Marco Prevedel-Slafter, Grace Tsiamis, Theo Tsiamis, Charlie Tsiamis and Jack Tsiamis; sister, Iris Bonsell; nephew, Kirk (Christy) Bonsell; and cousin, Harold Campbell.
He was preceded in death by his parents; grandparents; many aunts, uncles and cousins; sister-in-law, Linda (Bill) Duncan; and niece, Cara Bonsell.
A Celebration of a Life Well Lived will be held at the Rock Springs Holiday Inn, 1675 Sunset Drive, Rock Springs, on Tuesday, November 24, 2026, from 1 to 4 p.m.
Janis Michelle Boyd:
1962 – 2026
Janis Michelle Boyd was born July 13, 1962 and was adopted at birth by Dr. John Boyd and his wife Dorothy Boyd, of Cheyenne, Wyoming. She grew up in Cheyenne and spent all her years here. Jan graduated from East High School and enjoyed playing flute in the school band and also played the piano well.
Jan spent a number of years working in a medical clinic as part of the office staff. Then she worked many years in a trusted and capable position in an attorney’s office. Since about 2006 she worked for the State of Wyoming. In every position she was a valued employee.
In her late teens Jan studied the Bible and became one of Jehovah’s Witnesses. She served God faithfully the rest of her life, including almost 14 years with the local Spanish congregation of Witnesses.
Jan was known by her employers and by her friends for her loyalty, for her intelligence, her ability to be organized and to work well with all kinds of people. She was a treasured friend.
Jan is survived by a sister, Denise Vance, of Fort Worth, Texas.
A memorial service will be held at the Kingdom Hall of Jehovah’s Witnesses, 3422 North College Drive in Cheyenne, on Saturday, November 28 at 3:30 p.m
Gary R. Gross:
1952 – 2026
Gary R. Gross, born in 1952, died at home on November 2, 2026.
Throughout his lifetime, Gary enjoyed a myriad of activities, especially spending time with family and friends. He loved cooking (especially grilling meats and baking), gardening, RC racing, working with his hands, listening to all kinds of music, country western dancing, playing his dobro (inspired by Jerry Douglas), RV’ing, and going to bluegrass festivals.
Among his many favorite bluegrass bands were Union Station and The McCourys.
He was always up for a road trip.
Gary worked on a dairy farm, as a cook, a truck driver, a landscaper, and for the State of Wyoming. He loved being a camp host for national parks after his experience in Vermont.
He is survived by his wife, Charlynn Cariaso; two sons, Josh and his wife, Sarah, and Jonathan and his wife, Ann; a stepson, Alexander Bujak; and a stepdaughter, Kristina Hall, and her husband, Ashley.
He had nine grandchildren and one great-grandchild.
Gary loved dogs, and they all loved him.
