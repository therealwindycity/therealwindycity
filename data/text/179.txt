Sen.-elect Hageman eyes judicial, agency oversight
WORLAND — U.S. Rep. Harriet Hageman said that vetting federal judge appointments and department appointments was a main reason she opted to seek the open U.S. Senate seat for Wyoming rather than seek reelection to the U.S. House of Representatives.
In a telephone interview last week, Sen.-elect Hageman said once she realized the seat would be open with Sen. Cynthia Lummis opting not to seek reelection, she decided to run.
“There are several differences between the House and the Senate, and one of it relates specifically to my history and my work as a trial attorney. One of the things that the Senate does — and specifically the Judiciary Committee — is vetting judicial nominations,” she said.
“I was a trial attorney for 34 years. A lot of my work was in federal court. I have a pretty good idea of what makes a good judge, but I also have a pretty good idea of what makes a bad judge,” Hageman said. “And having the opportunity to actually question and engage with people who are seeking to be permanently ensconced in our judiciary, I think it gives me an opportunity to give Wyoming a very strong voice to make sure that we’re addressing like the Western issues that are so important to us, whether it is the management of federal lands or federal minerals ... the wild horses on (Bureau of Land Management) lands, all of those things. So having the opportunity to engage in the judicial nominating process is very important.”
She added that as a senator she would have the opportunity to query heads of federal agencies including the Environmental Protection Agency, Forest Service, Fish and Wildlife Service, BLM and Department of Agriculture.
“I will have the opportunity for any nominee to question them about their philosophy, and again, focus on those things, those issues, and those things that are really important to Wyoming,” she said.
Looking back at her service in the U.S. House of Representatives, Hageman said, “First of all, just getting to know the people in the House has really been very special, and it’s been such an honor to represent the state of Wyoming in the U.S. House of Representatives.
“Some of the things that I’m very proud of is the work that we have done on, say, case files for the citizens of Wyoming,” she continued. “We have resolved almost 6,000 cases on behalf of citizens, and we’ve returned over $18 million in the last three and a half years.”
Casework could include helping residents get their tax refund or contacting the Center for Medicare Services for citizen issues, contacting the Veterans Administration, and helping veterans to be able to get the services and benefits to which they are entitled.
“So those are some of the things that have been very important for me – is really to be a champion of Wyoming in every aspect, whether it is protecting our ag industry, advocating for our veterans, addressing healthcare, the One Big Beautiful Bill that we did,” Hageman said. “All of those things have been very important.”
Hageman addressed the following issues specifically in the nearly 30-minute interview:
How do you plan to work with a Democrat-controlled Congress?
“Well, the Democrats ran on this concept of affordability, you know, and they talked about gas prices and food prices and housing prices. I have been a champion of abundance not only since I’ve been in Congress, but pretty much my entire legal career. I mean I want to produce our own natural resources right here at home.
I’m very, very happy to work with the Democrats on their message of affordability, but what does that mean? That means we have to build the infrastructure to make sure that we can meet the needs and the demands of the American public.
If you want to make sure that we’ve that we’ve got affordable housing, then we need to better manage our national forests so that we’re not importing all of our lumber out of Canada, so that we can produce it domestically, and not only protect the industry, you know, our national forests, but grow those industries.
So I think that there’s a lot of things that I can work with the Democrats on if they are legitimately concerned about affordability and abundance, because we have a lot of infrastructure projects we need to build, and I think Wyoming is a wonderful place to do a lot of that work.”
Do you see an end to the Iran war?
“I do believe that the Iran conflict is winding down. One of the things that I have found of great interest over the last couple of weeks is the fact that by continuing the blockade and strangling them, they apparently had quite a bit of oil stockpiled in tankers around the world. They’re running out of that. That’s what has been funding the country.
But Iran has a lot more problems than just the issue of being able to get oil out ... The leaders, the political leaders, they actually moved out of Tehran into another area because they were running out of water. So there’s a lot of infrastructure issues in Iran, and hopefully they will be able to rebuild if and when the IRGC topples.
I think that the blockade has had a huge impact on their ability to generate revenue, and they were waiting until after the election as well. But the president has said that he’s going to stay the course in terms of continuing to prevent them from being able to sell oil, and that’s the only thing that they have.
So hopefully, we’re going to get some resolution to this in the next couple of months, and I hope that that resolution is freedom for the Iranian people.”
What would you say to people concerned about the software glitches that recently impacted air traffic, Microsoft users and banks?
“Well, one of the things that I was reading yesterday — and I don’t know that I have any more information than other people that are watching this with great interest — is that AI is actually operating to update these systems much more rapidly, and as a result, they’re running into glitches. We all know that those vulnerabilities are out there.
And as they are updating, they are in some ways creating new glitches ... So it’s in part, I think, that AI is finding more of these vulnerabilities, so we’re getting more updates. I think what I’m trying to say is that maybe this is a bit of a growing pain that we’re going through right now. Hopefully, we’ll get to a steady state fairly soon.”
Is there a message you would like to share?
“Well, I’d sure like to say thank you to everyone in the state of Wyoming for continuing to put their faith in me. I am extremely honored, as I said, to represent the state in the House, and I will continue to do the same kind of work in the Senate. I will continue to be a champion for Wyoming, including Washakie County. I will continue to be a champion for our industries, our citizens, our schools, our healthcare facilities, our farmers and ranchers, our small business owners. I will continue to do that. I will continue to be your voice back in Washington D.C.
There’s going to be a lot of work ahead of us, and there are going to be challenges in light of the election from a couple of weeks ago. But I’m ready for the battle, and I’m ready to continue to represent Wyoming.”
