Former Cheyenne nonprofit director charged with embezzling $10K in organization funds
CHEYENNE, Wyo. — A former local nonprofit director was arrested this month for reportedly embezzling over $10,000 from the organization’s bank account between 2024 and 2026. She was fired in July and now faces a felony charge.
Jesscialynn Burns, born in 1981, was charged with one count of felony theft, a crime punishable by up to 10 years in prison. She has not had a preliminary hearing to bind her case to the Laramie County District Court scheduled as of the morning of Wednesday, Nov. 18. She is presumed innocent until proven or pleading otherwise.
According to Cheyenne police, law enforcement was first notified of suspicious activity in early August when the director of a local nonprofit organization, Threads of Hope, called to report embezzlement by the organization’s former director, Burns.
Threads of Hope is a nonprofit organization dedicated to children in the foster care system. According to
the organization’s mission statement on its website
, the group strives to provide kids with “the essentials and more as they enter a new home, start school or age out of the system and begin a new stage in life.”
The new director told police that the organization’s board became suspicious of Burns almost immediately after she was fired in July. They reportedly found over $5,000 in unapproved spending by Burns across multiple transactions after a thorough search of their financial records.
A deeper dive into Threads of Hope’s records shows that Burns was issued two cards attached to the organization’s bank account. Records reportedly showed that, between 2024 and 2026, Burns used those cards to purchase groceries, fast food, dog food, cigars, cologne and nails, police said. Burns was also reportedly captured on video making some of these purchases, and purchases made on websites like Amazon and Sam’s Club were done through her own website accounts.
While Threads of Hope does provide clothing and occasionally baby formula to children in foster care, it does not provide them with groceries or any of the other things police said Burns purchased.
Between the opening of the organization’s bank account in June 2024 and Burns’ termination in July 2026, there was reportedly $10,328 worth of unauthorized purchases made directly from her cards.
That number does not include purchases made for things that could be interpreted as being for the foster children under her care, including things like diapers and kids’ clothing.
