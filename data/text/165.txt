Obituaries: Hayes; Rust
Ella Marie Hayes:
1933 – 2026
Ella Marie Hayes, 93, passed away peacefully on November 21, 2026, leaving behind a lifetime of love laughter, adventures, and enough family photographs to keep several generations entertained.
Ella Marie was born November 7, 1933, in Milliken, Colorado, to John and Ella Spomer. She grew up on the family farm just outside of Milliken, alongside her younger sister, Susanne, and cousin, Joyce Ann, who was raised as a sister. Following the tragic death of her father in an automobile accident, her uncle and cousin moved to the farm to help the family.
Ella Marie attended Daniels School, a rural schoolhouse near Milliken, and demonstrated a remarkable talent for academics. She graduated from high school at just 16 and earned her home economics degree from Colorado State University in Fort Collins at the age of 19. Throughout her entire education, she received only one grade below an A: in swimming. Despite her many talents, she never did learn to swim.
While attending college, Ella Marie’s sorority sisters played matchmaker, setting her up with Noel Van Hayes, known to everyone as Van. The introduction was clearly a success. They married and began a life together filled with family, friendships, and new adventures as Van’s career with the USDA brought opportunities in communities throughout Wyoming.
As Van advanced in his career, the family made their homes in Cheyenne, Wheatland, Meeteetse, and Lander, welcoming four children – David, Steven, Michael, and Katherine – along the way. Each move brought new friendships and experiences, but it was Saratoga, where they settled in 1969, that became their forever home.
Wherever they lived, Ella Marie created a warm and welcoming home. She was active in the Lutheran church, worked as a substitute teacher, and loved gardening, writing, and spending time with family.
Ella Marie had a gift for telling stories, both in person and in print. She was a prolific contributor of travel and event articles to The Fence Post magazine and other publications. She and Van shared a love of travel, exploring the United States, Canada, and Mexico with their fifth-wheel trailer, including a memorable journey to Alaska. They made a wonderful team, with Ella Marie writing about their adventures and Van providing photographs to accompany her articles. Together, they shared their experiences with readers and created a lasting record of their travels.
She was also the family’s unofficial photographer, videographer, and historian. No family gathering was complete without Ella Marie behind the camera, carefully documenting the occasion, sometimes to the mild annoyance of those being photographed. Today, those photographs and videos are among the family’s greatest treasures.
Family was always at the heart of Ella Marie’s life, and nowhere was that more evident than in her devotion to her grandchildren. She absolutely doted on them. Katherine’s children lived nearby and spent countless hours at Grandma’s house, where they were always welcome. She was like a second mother to David’s daughter, Michele Hayes Rupprecht, sharing a particularly close and loving bond. Whether offering encouragement, a helping hand, or simply a place to feel loved, Ella Marie was always there for her children and grandchildren. As her family grew to include great-grandchildren, that same love extended to another generation.
For 45 years, Saratoga was the center of Ella Marie and Van’s lives, a place where they built lasting friendships, raised their family, and enjoyed retirement. In 2014, changing health needs prompted a move to Cheyenne, bringing them closer to family and medical care. Though they left Saratoga, it would always hold a special place in their hearts. Van passes away in March 2020 at the age of 91.
Ella Marie is survived by her four children: David Hayes of Milliken, Colorado; Steven (Beverly) Hayes of Steamboat Springs, Colorado; Michael (Sherry) Hayes of Cheyenne, Wyoming; and Katherine Hayes Hall of Cheyenne, Wyoming.
She is also survived by her sister, Susanne (Sue) Stephens; and her cousin, Joyce Ann Einsphar.
She leaves behind eight grandchildren: Michele (Phil). Sarah (Mitch), Shawn, Jennifer (Deke), Daniel (Nicole), Brian (Elizabeth), Thomas (Lacy), and Matthew (Misty); and fourteen great-grandchildren: Rock, Elle, Aubrey, Avery, Gemma, Hunter, Peter, Astrid, Andrew, Harper, Hayden, Teagan, Parker, and Fenrir, with another eagerly awaited.
She was preceded in death by her parents, John and Ella Spomer; her beloved husband, Noel Van Hayes; and her son-in-law, Randy Hall.
Ella Marie will be remembered for her kindness, intelligence, curiostiy, love of family, and enthusiasm for life’s adventures. She was a beautiful, caring woman who found joy in the people around her and left a lasting impression on those fortunate enough to know her.
And thanks to Ella Marie, there will always be plenty of pictures to remember them by.
Ella Marie will be deeply missed and lovingly remembered by her family and all who knew her.
Carol Rust:
d. 2026
It is with broken hearts that we announce the passing of Carol Ann Rust, age 60.
Carol was a one-of-a-kind women who left her mark on everyone fortunate enough to know her. She was funny, caring, stubborn in all the ways that made he who she was, and truly the life of the party. she had a way of bringing laughter into a room and creating memories that would last a lifetime.
Above all else, Carol loved her family. Her daughter, grandchildren, fiance, and fur babies meant the world to her. She cherished the moments spent together, the laughter, the conversations, and all the little things that made life special. Her love for her family was endless, and she will forever hold a special place in their hearts.
Carol is survived by her daughter, Jessica Bee, and her spouse, David Bee; her beloved grandchildren, Levi Rust and Lucas Bee; her parents, Jack and Lois Smith; her fiance, Dewey Reed; her niece, Alexandria Smith, and her partner, Tyler Cheslic; Alexandria’s children: Sophia, Nevaeh, and Clayton Smith; and Tyler’s daughter, Kaydence Cheslic.
Though our hearts are shattered by her unexpected passing, we find a little comfort in imagining that she is no longer alone. Carol has now been reunited in heaven with her beloved sister, who was waiting to welcome her home.
We find comfort in picturing the two sisters together again, holding hands, sharing laughter, and watching over the family they both loved so dearly. A reunion that brings comfort to those left behind, even as we wish more than anything that they could both still be here with us.
Carol’s time here may have ended far too soon, but her story doesn’t end here. She will live on through the memories, stories, laughter, and hearts of everyone who loved her.
Until we meet again, Carol. You are loved beyond words, missed beyond measure, and will never be forgotten.
