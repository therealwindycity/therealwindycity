Laramie County sending off $216K grant request to Meta for safer school crossings
by
Garrett Grochowski
November 18, 2026

CHEYENNE, Wyo. — Laramie County is submitting an application to receive more than $216,000 through Meta’s The Future is For Everyone Grant. With the money, the county said it would look to install new safety features at Arp and Afflerbach elementary schools.
During the Laramie County Board of Commissioners’ Nov. 17 meeting, the county approved submitting a handful of grant applications, requests for funding from various organizations for specific projects. Commissioners approved sending off requests for bulletproof vests for the Laramie County Sheriff’s Office and for funding for Laramie County Emergency Management.
The commissioners also approved the acceptance of grant funds that the county was awarded after submitting applications in the past. The county accepted a $50,000 grant award from the Cheyenne Community Recreation District to resurface the playground at the Archer Complex and a $450,000 DOJ Bureau of Justice Assistance grant award —requiring a $150,000 match — to fund the DUI Court program through 2030.
The commissioners also voted to send off a grant application to Meta,
the technology company currently establishing itself locally through the construction of a data center
.
Meta’s Future is For Everyone Fund was established in August 2026 to support the communities that Meta is building data centers in. Meta CEO and founder Mark Zuckerberg said in a letter the day the fund was announced that it is a financial commitment to Meta’s new equitable superintelligence philosophy that it plans to use to guide its relationships with its communities.
Cheyenne has already been the beneficiary of Meta dollars. The company financed 180 stormwater filtration devices for Cheyenne’s Crow Creek drainage in August. It also keeps a running list of other contributions made to the community, which can be read
here
.
What Laramie County is seeking is $216,500 for the project it calls Laramie County Safe Steps. With the money, the application reads, the county will install three pre-formed thermoplastic crosswalks, stop bars and five flashing beacon signs at key school crossings at two Cheyenne elementary schools: Afflerbach and Arp, the latter of which is preparing for a 2028 reopening.
The new safety features would make traveling to school safer for over 700 students living in neighborhoods where walking to school is the norm, the county wrote in its application. Those two schools in particular would benefit from the additional safety features due to their proximity to Meta’s and other companies’ data center projects in south Cheyenne.
Design work is scheduled for 2026 and 2027, with procurement and installation targeted for completion by the end of 2027.
