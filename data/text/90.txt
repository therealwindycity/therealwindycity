Dear Cheyenne,
I am writing in response to the article titled “‘A culture of total control’: Trial examines inner workings of the Wyoming Boys School”, published on your news site on Nov. 17, 2026.
As a Wyoming resident, I was unaware of the conditions that youth were facing in this correctional facility. It sounds a lot more like prison than somewhere young men can go to learn better behavior.
Dr. Jeider with Nevada Mental Health (2023) finds that juveniles who need these kinds of corrections should be treated differently than adults in the prison system. Rather than punishment such as shaved heads and limited social interaction, young people need psychological support and behavior modification. Kids should be in school so that they can learn and build support networks.
As a junior in high school, I did extensive research on juvenile recidivism and discovered similar patterns. The current system is not designed to benefit those who need it the most.
I hope that this school is one of many who might reform their organizations to produce more meaningful results and support the youth that will one day be the politicians, teachers, and leaders of our country.
Whitney Mead
Cheyenne
