Josephine Joyce Abreu:
1932 – 2026
September 6, 1932, the world became a lot brighter with the birth of Josephine (Joyce) Voelker one of six children (three boys and three girls), to Hester and Cecil Voelker of Halfmoon, Montana. She was the eldest of the girls, so needless to say in that age, she helped in mothering the other children, the oldest being Joe followed by James (whitey), Josephine, Janet, John, and finally Janice, the baby.
The family moved from Halfmoon, Montana to Yakima, Washington, where most of the family live to this day. Upon mom’s graduation from high school, she enlisted in the Air Force, with her first base assignment in the 50’s being F.E. Warren AFB Cheyenne Wyoming. One of her fondest memories of that being the meeting of none other than Roy Clark who, at the time, frequently played at the NCO club. Following that assignment, she was stationed at the Pentagon, where she worked for some high level generals. In 1954, she was asked if she would consider modeling with the Air Force’s new Stenafax, which was the copying machine of the day. Of course she did, and her photo was published in the Dun’s Review and modern industry, which was part of Dun and Bradstreet publications in December of 1954. All that knew her knew this was one of the highlights of her life.
Also In 1954, she meet a handsome officer flyboy navigator from Honolulu, Hawaii, named Ralph Abreu. They would be married December 28, 1954. In those days if an NCO wanted to marry an officer, one or the other had to get out of the service. So on January 13, 1955, she separated from the Air Force. And she began her life as an officer’s wife. They were stationed at Rapid City, South Dakota when baby number one came along: Wayne Allen Abreu. And, to this day, he lets you know he was number one. From there, they went to Dyess AFB in Abilene, Texas, where baby number two came along: Tanya Ann Abreu. But for the best baby, they had to move to Fairbanks, Alaska; baby number three: Denis Abel Abreu. That’s what happens when they want you to write the obituary. Writers freedom, I think they call it. That’s what you get when your sister and her friends dress you up like a girl and you cant do anything about it!
So that’s it for the babies, then we were off to Omaha Nebraska to Offutt AFB where dad continued navigating the KC-135 stratotanker as well as the AWACS version.
After four years there, we were off to Barksdale AFB in Shreveport, Louisiana. All this time, mom was not only raising us, but she was being the dutiful, officer’s wife, officer’s wives’ club etc. She had her hands full. In the military, as your husband achieves more rank, more is expected of the wife, such as welcoming new officers’ wives, and their families, and showing them how things are done, and what will be expected of them. And mom was good at it. Mom was the one forming us into young men and women, which was no small feat. As I was always into something, or bringing some kind of creature into the house, hoping the parents would let me keep it, Wayne was busy into sports, and Tanya, well she was doing girly stuff. This is when Junior came into all our lives. Junior became the family cat, that would end up traveling all over the world with us. Mom swore to God, that he was her dad reincarnated.
Four years was up again, and dad became a full Colonel, and now he would be flying a desk at none other than Karamursel, Turkey, where I’m sure most of the readers of this obituary meet mom, as dad was supposed to be the Deputy Commander of the base, but after a short period of time, the base commander was transferred and dad became base commander. Mom was a lovely woman who was a friend to any and all of our friends, because she spent so much time with the base children, whether it was chaperoning them on field trips, skiing trips, or something through the youth center, on top of welcoming dignitaries from Turkey leadership, or other countries.
It’s 1975, and we are moving to F.E. Warren AFB, Cheyenne, Wyoming. In 1975, dad was chosen to be 1976 Military Chairman for Cheyenne Frontier Days. Since it was the Bicentennial, CFD decked out the husband and wives of the committee from head to toe with red, white, and blue. Mom loved representing the Air Force and its families. By now Wayne was off to college in Lake Charles Louisiana. Tanya just graduated Central High School. I was 12 years old. 1977 brought dads retirement from the Air Force, as well as mom’s divorce from dad. Her and I were on our own. I’m pretty sure she was scared to death, for she hadn’t worked a job for 20+ years and she had a 12-year-old to boot.
Luckily, mom got a job at Dr. Hickman and Dr. Kincheloe dentist here in Cheyenne as a receptionist and did so for 20-plus years. Thankfully, she let me join the boy scouts, so I was camping about every weekend. She did keep after me to get my eagle scout, I will give her that, cause without her, I probably would not have completed it, least she reminded me of that until the end. I had Joined the Air Force in 1984, and was stationed in Charleston South Carolina. One day in 1985, I received a phone call from my mom in hysterics, I thought, one of my siblings had passed. But come to find out she had accidentally ran over our family cat, Junior. She never forgave herself for this.
During her time at the dentist office, she befriended a fine gentleman named Phillip Kalber, that later became what she called her “significant.” The two of them would be inseparable for over 25 years, longer than she had been with dad. He took her all over the world; they went on far too many cruises to list here. They also belonged to the paddle/riverboat society. They had been down and up the Mississippi so many times, they took the paddleboat to Alaska on more than one occasion. They took the paddleboat down the Rhine River in Germany. Phillip worshiped the ground mom walked on, as well as she did of him. I was so glad she meet Phil, they were so deserving of one another. The day mom lost him, on Nov. 21, 2006, I think she in a way, knew things in her life would never be even remotely the same. I mean who could follow Phil Kalber. I’m so glad the two of them got to experience each other.
In 2008, mom started experiencing heart issues, and I was having to take so much time off work to get her to appointments in Denver, as Cheyenne had no device cardiologist. I decided to quit my job that took me all over the state of Wyoming, to be close enough to care for her, should she need it. In 2018, we sold her tri-level house, as she could no longer manage the stairs, as well as having to endure numerous health issues. Gaye and I moved out of our rental we had just up the street from her, and we bought a house together with her.
In her time at the new house, she endured a lot of health issues, until her passing in her sleep, Friday the 13th, 2026. But don’t get me wrong, she had so many great memories here at the house, spending time with her grown children, sons and daughters-in-law, grandchildren, great -grandchildren, and friends. Mom was a friend to all that she knew and some that she didn’t. I know that her only regret was that she had out-lived the man that she so loved, and her friends, except for one, Rosemary Peterson from Florida, who was a neighbor of ours at Offutt AFB in Omaha. I know she will miss her friendship and talks.
Josephine was preceded in death by her father, Cecil; mother, Hester; niece, Zoe Mary; brothers, Joe and Jim; sister, Janet; her significant, Phillip Kalber; her sister, Janice’s significant, Lloyd Septa; her beloved cat, Junior; and her ex-husband, Ralph Abreu
Mom is survived by her baby brother, John (Frankie); baby sister, Janice (Lloyd); Conrad Wingerter (brother-in-law); son, Wayne Abreu (Josie); daughter, Tanya Porter (Dale); son, Denis Abreu (Gaye); grandchildren: Paul Bell (Caitlin), Megan (Luke), Andrew (Jaclyn), Sarah Abreu, Ryan Abreu, Brett Hoberg (Billie), LaChelle Hoberg; 16 great-grandchildren; and three great-great-grandchildren. along with numerous nieces and nephews whom she adored, as well as they adored her.
Mom called me and my wife Gaye her angels, but it was her that was the angel for so many, throughout her years. She will be so sorely missed.
We love you, and will miss you terribly.
Sharon Elizabeth Baccei:
1940 – 2026
Sharon Elizabeth Baccei, went to be with the lord on Monday afternoon, Nov. 16, 2026, while in hospice care in Cheyenne, Wyoming. Sharon was born in Cheyenne to Lorene Elizabeth Berkley and Howard Foster Smith in 1940. Except for a short time in Denver as an infant, Sharon lived in Cheyenne her entire life. Her parents divorced when she was a baby and she was raised by her mother and grandmother.
She had no siblings and no cousins. When she was still in high school, she started riding horses at a local stable. This quickly evolved into Sharon and her mother opening their own boarding stable. Soon after that she got the concession to operate the stables at the F.E. Warren Air Force Base in Cheyenne. At the base Sharon met a young airman named Joe Baccei. He was good with horses and when he got out of the Air Force she hired him.
In 1964, they married and the next year they had Lisa. Lisa was followed by Lynne two years later and Leah eight years after Lynne. Sharon and Joe and their daughters ran the stable for more than three decades until the program was discontinued by the Air Force in the mid 1990’s. They had as many as 150 horses at one point. Sharon was very proud of her horses and rode sidesaddle in the Cheyenne Frontier Days parade for many years.
In 1966, they bought a place north of Cheyenne that they called the Diamond B Ranch and ran sheep there until 2021. Joe passed away three years ago and Sharon vowed to never leave the ranch. She was true to her word and stayed there under the care of her daughter, Lynne, until just a few days ago. Sharon leaves three daughters, four grandchildren, and many friends with happy memories of wonderful times riding on the Wyoming prairie with Sharon.
Sharon will be interred with Joe at Cheyenne National Cemetery.
Wesley Wade Barkell:
1960 – 2026
Wesley Wade Barkell, 66, of Pine Bluffs, Wyoming, passed away unexpectedly on November 17, 2026.
Born on May 26, 1960, to Larry and Joyce Barkell, Wade grew up in Pine Bluffs and graduated from Pine Bluffs High School. He began his trucking career with Sanders Gravel in Pine Bluffs, starting a career that would span his entire adult life. Truck driving wasn’t just what Wade did for a living. It was a part of who he was, and he continued doing what he knew and loved until his final day. But Wade’s life was about so much more than the miles he traveled.
Wade loved good food and especially enjoyed cooking. He loved riding his Harley, and more recently, he found enjoyment in tinkering with his remote-control trucks. An avid Broncos fan, he never missed his Sunday $20 bet. He treasured camping at the lake, in the mountains and traveling, making memories along the way. Wade was a natural problem-solver who could fix just about anything and had the ability to drive just about anything. Whether behind the wheel, working with his hands, or enjoying time doing the things he loved, Wade had a way of making the most of life. Faith was also an important part of Wade’s life. He began each day with a word of prayer and always made sure to pray before meals. His faith was woven into the everyday moments of his life and was an important part of who he was.
Wade was a man who never hesitated to lend a helping hand. No matter what was happening in his own life, he always made time for someone who needed him. Whether it was a small favor or something that required hours of his time, Wade showed up, usually with a smile on his face and never expecting anything in return.That was simply Wade.He was dependable, hardworking, generous, and kind. He was someone you could count on, someone whosepresence will be deeply missed.
Wade is survived by his loving wife, Elizabeth; his parents, Larry and Joyce Barkell; his sidekick, Rocket; his sister, Lynn (Steve); nephew, Jeff (Natalie) and family; niece, Trisha (Brian) and family; nephews, Tanner and Ruger and their families; as well as his extended Pine Bluffs Sand and Gravel family, who were such an important part of his life. He was preceded in death by his brother, Gerald Patrick; his sister-in-law, Lillian Ortivez (godmother); his grandparents; and his in-laws.
Wade’s unexpected passing leaves an emptiness that words cannot express. He will forever be remembered for his generous heart, his willingness to help others, and the smile he carried through life.
In lieu of flowers, memorial donations may be made at the
Cheyenne Animal Shelter
in Wade’ memory.
The roads he traveled were many, the lives he touched were countless, and the memories he leaves behind will last forever.
James Joseph “Jimmy” Gregorio:
1947 – 2026
James Joseph “Jimmy” Gregorio, 79, made his way home to the lord on November 19, 2026 at Cheyenne Regional Medical Center.
Jimmy was born September 8, 1947 in Mount Vernon, New York, to Michael and Francis Gregorio. He married Helen Espinoza on December 27, 1971.
Mr. Gregorio worked as a painter and construction tradesman. He also volunteered at St. Joseph’s Food Pantry. Jimmy loved music, especially Motown and the blues. He cherished a cruise around town jamming to his tunes or dancing to his favorite songs at family gatherings. Funny and unfiltered, he was often the life of the party, making everyone laugh.
Jimmy loved his three sons with all his heart. He spent countless hours teaching them the tricks of his trade, and each of them worked with him at various job sites through the years. Another of Jimmy’s passions was working on cars with his boys, and he had fun teaching them to turn wrenches and use scrap parts in inventive ways. He was a lover of abandoned treasures and once had collections of rocks, old driftwood, statues, cars, and figurines. There was no greater love for Jimmy than his grandchildren, though. They always received his undivided attention and he took great interest in whatever they were doing.
Jimmy’s generosity was unparalleled. He would give anyone his time, the last dollar in his pocket, or the shirt off his back. He never had much in his life, but whatever he did have, he shared. He was truly a gift to his family and to this world.
Jimmy is survived by his children: James David (Irene), Richard Michael (Wendy), David Anthony (Amber); 12 grandchildren; and six great-grandchildren. He is also survived by many siblings, nieces, and nephews.
His family takes comfort in knowing that Jimmy has now been reunited with the love of his life, his beautiful wife, Helen; along with his parents, Michael and Francis; and three brothers: Danny, Frank, and Vincent.
Our family would like to acknowledge James, Irene, and Gabriel for opening their home to Jimmy. Caregiving is not easy; we are so grateful for everything they did to keep Jimmy healthy, happy, and comfortable in his final years.
In lieu of flowers, and in the spirit of Jimmy’s generous nature, the family asks that donations be made to St. Joseph’s Catholic Church or St. Joseph’s Food Pantry.
Robert “Bobby Gray” J. Gray:
1958 – 2026
Robert “Bobby Gray” J. Gray, of Cheyenne, Wyoming, passed away peacefully at home on November 20, 2026. He was born on July 3, 1958, in Greeley, Colorado, and lived a life marked by hard work, a generous heart, a wonderful sense of humor, and a deep love for the people and animals who crossed his path.
Bobby was never one to be defined by formal education. Instead, he was a man who learned through life, hard work, and experience. In 1977, he answered the call to serve his country by joining the United States Army, where he proudly served until his honorable discharge on July 8, 1988. Following his military service, Bobby worked as a truck driver and as a roughneck in the oil fields, earning his living through determination, grit, and a willingness to do whatever needed to be done.
Above all else, Bobby will be remembered for the kind of man he was. Deeply kind, thoughtful, and considerate, he had a heart that seemed to have room for everyone. He was the type of person who would stop to save a rabbit on the side of the road or welcome a complete stranger to sit down and share a hot meal. He had a special compassion for those who were struggling and never hesitated to lend a helping hand. His kindness was not something he simply talked about; it was something he lived every day.
Bobby also had an incredible sense of humor and a gift for making people laugh. He could bring a little lightness into even the hardest moments and had a way of making people feel welcome and at ease. Beneath that humor was a romantic heart and a man who cherished the connections he made with others. He was a true people person who loved deeply and found joy in being surrounded by family and friends.
He was especially proud of his involvement in helping rebuild the American Legion here in Cheyenne, a contribution that reflected his dedication to his fellow veterans and his desire to give something meaningful back to his community.
When he wasn’t working or spending time with loved ones, Bobby found happiness in the simple pleasures of life. He loved animals, especially dogs, and enjoyed being outdoors, camping, and fishing. He also had a natural talent for making things, finding satisfaction in using his hands and creating something from what he had. Whether through his skills, his generosity, or simply his willingness to show up for someone, Bobby had a way of making a difference.
Bobby was preceded in death by his niece, Christyn J. Gray; his older brother, Richard Gray Jr.; his nephews, Timothy Casares and Anthony Casares; and his parents, Richard Gray and Martha A. Gray.
He leaves behind his loving wife of 34 years, Debbie Gray, with whom he shared a lifetime of memories and love. He is also survived by his siblings: Rosemary Gray, Sally Gray, Nash Gray (Tammy), and Christopher Gray (Amy); his sons, Josh A. Gray (Maureen) and Jerrid R. Gray; his stepchildren, Jami A. Evans (Gabe) and Cory J. Rusch; and his cherished grandchildren, Caleb Evans and Andrew Evans.
Though Bobby may no longer be here to share a laugh, lend a helping hand, tell a story, or make someone smile, the kindness he showed and the memories he created will continue to live on in the hearts of those who knew and loved him.
A life is not measured only by the years we are given, but by the lives we touch along the way. Bobby touched many through his generosity, his humor, his compassion, and his unmistakable way of making people feel like they mattered.
He was a husband, father, grandfather, brother, veteran, friend, and, above all, a good man with a big heart. He will be remembered not for what he had, but for what he gave of himself to others.
He will be loved always, remembered forever, and deeply missed.

Sandra Diane McNulty:
1948 – 2026
‘Tis a sad day in Cheyenne, Wyoming. I need to report a tragic event today: the passing of Sandi McNulty (née Mellies), 78, on November 20, 2026. She has gone to heaven with the blessings of family and friends at her home. She put up a great fight against Alzheimer’s, but as it does for so many who have lived with this terrible disease, it ultimately won.
She will be missed by all who knew her. Sandi never met a stranger. She loved animals, reading, sewing projects, cooking, gardening, and dancing. Sandi worked many jobs during her life. Her favorite was at Natrona County High School (NCHS). She loved helping the students find what they were looking for and talking with them.
Sandi was born in Great Bend, Kansas, in June 1948. When she was 12, her family moved to Casper, Wyoming. She graduated from NCHS in 1966 and then went on to get an associates degree at Casper College. While there, she was awarded the honor of being in Who’s Who Among Students in American Junior Colleges.
Sandi met Mike, the love of her life, in 1967. They were married in 1969. Eight years later they welcomed their son, Matthew. They lived in Casper until 1997, when they moved to Cheyenne. Sandi worked at Anderson Elementary School for a while, then went on to the Wyoming Tribune-Eagle, and finally to United Medical Center, where she retired.
Sandi was preceded in death by her parents; sister; two brothers; two nieces; and multiple aunts and uncles. She is survived by her husband, Mike; son, Matthew; daughter-in-law, Lori; and grandchildren: Quinnten, Camille, Lilly, and Gavin.
In lieu of flowers, please donate to her favorite charity, Black Dog Animal Rescue (bdar.org).
A celebration of life will be held December 17, 2026, 10 a.m., at Wiederspahn Radomsky Chapel of the Chimes. A reception will follow the service at The Gathering Place.
Carolyn Ann Webber:
1943 – 2026
Carolyn Ann Webber, of Cheyenne was promoted to glory on November 17, 2026 in Cheyenne after living with dementia for several years.
Born May 7, 1943 in Riverton, Wyoming, Carolyn was chosen to be the only child of Leonard and Lola Carlson, and lived her entire life in Cheyenne. Following her graduation as part of the first class of Cheyenne East High School, she worked as a secretary at The Salvation Army and that is where she met her husband, Jim Webber. The couple had two daughters and were married for 61 years before Jim was promoted to glory. During that time, The Salvation Army was a very significant part of their lives as the place they attended church, served in ministry roles, and volunteered for programs serving the community. Carolyn was well known as the pianist, and continued to play until the week before her death.
Carolyn served in other community organizations as well, including Civil Air Patrol, Oak Spokes, Cheyenne Boat & Ski Club, and Friendship Force. She worked as a secretary for a number of years and used those skills to help the clubs she served in. She transitioned to a second career when she and a partner purchased a tax preparation business named American Tax Associates. Carolyn continued to work at the company for a few years even after she and her partner sold the business.
Those who knew Carolyn will remember her as a sweet, kind person who was willing to help others in many ways. Before and after having two children of her own, Carolyn often helped care for children and foreign exchange students. She also cared for elderly people, visiting nursing homes, where she played piano and led church services, as well as driving people to appointments and assisting disabled acquaintances. Carolyn was always willing to lend a hand and practice hospitality. Family was very important to her. She loved travel and camping. She was business-minded, but also creative and musical. She never passed up a puzzle, took lots of pictures, and always enjoyed reminiscing with pictures. Carolyn shared her faith by demonstrating trust in God and showing His love to all those around her.
Carolyn is survived by her daughters, Barbara (Mark) Brennan and Lynada Spencer as well as Barbara’s children: Michael “Jori” (Rachel) Eyres, Josh (Danielle) Eyres, Jeff (Hazuki) Brennan, Andrew (Julia) Brennan, Lillie-Ann Brennan; and Lynada’s children: Cody (Zoey) Spencer, Jeremiah Spencer, and Raylin (Zac) Ragsdale; as well as 11 great-grandchildren.
She is preceded in death by her husband, James Webber; and parents, Leonard and Lola Carlson.
A Celebration of Life will be held at The Salvation Army, 601 E 20th St, at noon on Saturday, November 28, 2026. A light lunch and fellowship time will follow the service.
Donations in Carolyn’s memory may be made to the Cheyenne Corps of The Salvation Army.
