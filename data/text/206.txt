Start a Holiday Tradition: Enjoy $1 Christmas trees year-round from BLM Wyoming
WYOMING — The Bureau of Land Management is offering Christmas tree and firewood permits for $1 year-round in Wyoming, giving families an affordable way to find and cut their own Christmas tree or firewood and spend time together on public lands.
“Bring your family or invite a friend to cut their first Christmas tree,” BLM Director Steve Pearce said. “Years from now, you’ll remember the adventure you shared on your public lands.”
The BLM’s All Americans Outside initiative encourages people to explore and enjoy their public lands. These affordable permits give families a simple way to take part and spend time outdoors together.
These prices apply in areas where BLM offers personal-use Christmas tree or fuelwood permits.
To purchase a permit online, visit
forestproducts.blm.gov
and select the state and field office where you plan to cut. If you don’t see a permit for your area online, visit BLM’s Forest and Wood Product Permits page to find your local office and ask about permits available over the counter.
Personal-use fuelwood permits are also available for $1 per cord year-round, helping Wyoming households stock up on wood to keep their homes warm through winter.
Be advised that permits come with a list of special stipulations such as height restrictions and no cutting of five-needle pines. Currently, the Cody Field Office is not selling permits for Christmas trees or fuelwood due to inaccessibility. The Newcastle Field Office offers tree permits at the front desk, but fuelwood permits can be found online. Requirements can differ between districts so before heading out, review local cutting-area maps and permit requirements, check access and weather conditions, and bring your permit. Then enjoy the holiday adventure!
The BLM manages about 245 million acres of public land located primarily in 12 western states, including Alaska, on behalf of the American people. The BLM also administers 700 million acres of sub-surface mineral estate throughout the nation. Our mission is to sustain the health, diversity, and productivity of America’s public lands for the use and enjoyment of present and future generations.
