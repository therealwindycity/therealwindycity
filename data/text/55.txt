North Cheyenne neighborhood to fill in with more houses, apartments upon plat approvals next week
by
Garrett Grochowski

Two empty parcels are being planned for residential development, with the Cheyenne City Council to vote on final plats next week (Google Maps screenshot)
CHEYENNE, Wyo. — A north Cheyenne neighborhood, nearly complete with houses in all but two vacant tracts, will further condense if the Cheyenne City Council approves two final plats next week. The proposed developments, looked over by the Public Services Committee on Monday, would add an additional 142 housing units to the area.
The bulk of the units — 130 of them — would go up in a large lot called the Dell Range Addition. The subdivision abuts
Ridge Road and Thomas Road
. According to plans shared by City of Cheyenne staff, 69 total lots would make up this new neighborhood. 46 of those lots would be single-family townhomes, 21 would become multi-family dwellings and the remaining two would be used exclusively for drainage and open space. Each of the 21 multi-family buildings would contain four units.
Dell Range Addition plat (City of Cheyenne)
Plans for this area, which have been in development for a while, reflect a smaller amount of homes than what developers had originally imagined. Cheyenne planning staff noted the proposal that made it to the Public Services Committee actually represents a reduction of 100 housing units compared to an earlier concept for the parcel that included only multi-family buildings.
The city had previously passed on the original, denser designs due to concerns of compatibility with the surrounding neighborhoods. The developer’s solution was to convert plans for dense housing on the western side of the subdivision, where it meets single-family, ranch-style homes, into a “buffer” row of the same type of houses. That way, the people already living in the western neighborhood won’t have to worry about neighbors in a multi-floor complex looking into their backyards.
The second subdivision, called Pronghorn Crossing, is a replat of a previously submitted subdivision idea and introduces an additional 12 housing units — all of which will be for Habitat for Humanity homes — into the area south of Storey Boulevard.
Pronghorn Crossing plat (City of Cheyenne)
Because the houses will be built for low-income residents with the goal of them keeping the houses long-term, the applicants had to make some special considerations and tweak some of their original plans. Habitat for Humanity said it chose to replat from a row of condos into 12 fee-simple cottage lots so low-income buyers wouldn’t face monthly HOA maintenance fees. Instead, the organization will handle common-area upkeep internally using mortgage proceeds.
That, and a unique deed and shared depreciation model, should keep those homes affordable to both their first generations of occupants and future buyers.
Both of these plats were supported by the Public Services Committee, meaning they only need approval by the Cheyenne City Council next week to get the green light. Development is reportedly already in progress at Pronghorn Crossing, but the larger neighborhood will need more approvals before work can truly begin. Developers will meet before the Cheyenne Board of Adjustment in December regarding which direction the multi-family buildings’ main entrances should face, the street or the parking lot planned for the other side.
