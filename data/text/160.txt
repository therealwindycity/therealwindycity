Shop local, Cheyenne: Small Business Saturday supports community, local economy
CHEYENNE, Wyo. — With Christmas fast approaching, Cheyenne residents are preparing for a busy weekend of shopping. They will line up outside the big-box retailers on Friday. They’ll be filling their cart online on Cyber Monday. Small Cheyenne businesses, meanwhile, hope that shoppers will come to their local stores participating in Small Business Saturday.
Small Business Saturday, established by credit card company American Express in 2010, advocates for holiday shoppers to go to local stores to capitalize on the busiest shopping day of the year and support their local economy. Since it was founded, it’s picked up support from the Small Business Administration and looks to rival the big shopping days it’s sandwiched between.
Communities across the country now make it a priority to show some support for local businesses the Saturday after Thanksgiving. Among those communities is Cheyenne.
“You’re supporting these local business owners. You’re supporting local property owners. You’re supporting the people of your community that own these stores and that are passionate about the same things that we’re all passionate about,” Brandon Unruh, Cheyenne Downtown Development Authority’s new executive director, said. “Supporting them is important from a community perspective.”
According to the United States Chamber of Commerce
, there are more than 33 million small businesses in the United States. Whether at brick-and-mortar locations across the country or within the digital realm, they are fractured and without the advertising budgets that help larger chains earn deal-hungry holiday patrons. Despite that, they constitute around 40% of the United States’ annual GDP and the holiday season remains their most important time of the year. According to the chamber’s
Small Business Index
, 79% of small business owners say that the holiday season is critical to their work.
For the American government, Small Business Saturday is as much about honoring the hard work of small business owners as it is about supporting them financially.
“By designating a day in their honor, we are reminded that these businesses rely on our patronage during critical consumer shopping seasons as well as year-round,” the Chamber of Commerce said.
The chamber added that local stores offer more personalized, one-of-a-kind pieces that really embody a community. That, Unruh said, makes for some thoughtful gifts.
“You can get some fantastic products, and everybody’s going to shop in a bunch of different places when they’re looking for for holiday items, decorations, presents, and things like that,” he said. “But if you want something that’s unique — and I think people are always trying to put as much as possible into into gifts and how they decorate their home — you’re not going to find any better products than when you shop local. That’s the great thing about Cheyenne’s downtown: You’ve got a wide variety of shops that you can check out and you’re going to find something special and uniquely Wyoming and uniquely Cheyenne here.”
Shopping local, besides supporting small business owners, helps support local infrastructure. Every time a consumer buys an item in a local store rather going down to Colorado or elsewhere, they contribute to their local and state sales tax.
In Laramie County, starting Jan. 1, shoppers will pay 6% of what their purchase cost on sales tax
. 4% goes to the State of Wyoming, 1% goes to Laramie County and pays for critical infrastructure like roads.
Another 1% funds different local projects specifically voted on by county residents in August.
Even though purchases made in big box stores in Cheyenne or made online by Cheyenne residents collect sales tax the same way shopping local does, money spent in local stores travels a little further in extra ways. Cheyenne Mayor Patrick Collins, who owned a small business in Cheyenne for four decades, explained that revenue from purchases made at local stores goes into the pocket of a community member, rather than to a national chain’s headquarters. That’s more money that that store owner can put directly back into the local economy.
“When people shop locally, that let us be able to hire it and keep our employees, to be able to put it back into the community. It’s important year round, but it’s especially important as we think about Black Friday,” Collins said. “The importance of that, to try to find an excuse to shop in one of the local businesses, I think it makes a huge difference.”
Cheyenne’s smooth roads, its new fire trucks and police vehicles, its library and other public buildings — they all partly have local stores and their patrons to thank.
Cheyenne is a community of a little more than 65,000 people, but it punches well above its weight in local shopping experiences. See
