Gordon stalls $13M grant to trona company that’s cutting 350 jobs in southwest Wyoming
by
Dustin Bleizeffer
,
WyoFile
Gov. Mark Gordon will take “no action” for now on a $13 million state taxpayer-funded grant request by WE Soda, the London-based trona miner and soda ash producer that plans to cut 274 workers in southwest Wyoming beginning in January and a total 350 over the next two years.
“The conversation has changed, as a whole, from when this [Wyoming Energy Dominance Fund] application was first submitted,” Gordon said in a prepared statement Monday. “Before committing state severance tax resources, we need to have a better understanding of what the overall conditions this critical industry is facing, and what the future may look like.”
WE Soda announced Nov. 4 it would
reduce its Wyoming workforce from about 850 to 500 over the next two years
, primarily due to its new plan to shift its Westvaco trona mine from underground mining to drill-and-pump solution mining.
WE Soda workers wait at a bus stop in Rock Springs for a ride to work their shifts at the company’s trona mine and soda ash operations. (Dustin Bleizeffer/WyoFile)
Prior to the announced layoffs, the company applied for — and the Wyoming Energy Authority recommended — a
$13 million grant from the state’s new Energy Dominance Fund
. WE Soda said it would use the money to build a “high-pressure natural gas supply connection from the Williams’ MountainWest Overthrust Pipeline system” to its Westvaco operations, an investment that’s not immediately tied to its plan shift to solution mining.
The union representing workers at WE Soda’s trona and soda ash operations — United Steelworkers Local 13214 —
rallied workers and the community
to implore Wyoming officials for help and for Gordon to pump the brakes on the grant and to instead “find a way to support the workers who made that corporation successful.”
Many southwest Wyoming leaders and residents echoed the sentiment.
The Brewery in Green River. (Dustin Bleizeffer/WyoFile)
“The whole thing sucks,” a Green River bar manager told WyoFile on Thursday. She didn’t want to share her name for fear of retribution toward workers and local businesses. “I don’t think the state should subsidize an industry that is depleting our job market. We struggle with that anyway. This is going to make it worse.
“WE Soda,” she added, “can take a flying fuck into a rolling donut.”
The Energy Authority, which administers the state’s
Energy Dominance Fund
, told WyoFile last week that it had recommended the $13 million grant be approved before it learned of the company’s plans for major job cuts.
Gordon on Monday directed the agency “to conduct an expedited thorough examination of the economic impacts to Sweetwater County and the state from the shifting global dynamics affecting Wyoming trona production.”
The Energy Authority “stands ready to deliver on Gov. Gordon’s request to examine how this global market is changing and what those changes could mean for Wyoming,” Energy Authority Executive Director Rob Creager said in a prepared statement. “This work will give the state a stronger foundation for evaluating investments and identifying where support can make a lasting difference.”
A handful of companies mine trona in southwest Wyoming and process it — on site — into soda ash, the primary material used to make glass, myriad household products and industrial products such as large batteries. Wyoming once held about 14% of the global soda ash market worth some $1.3 billion in exports, and its
operations directly employed some 2,500 workers
in 2025.
Shifting Westvaco operations from underground mining to drill-and-pump “solution” recovery is necessary, WE Soda has said, due to market pressures from China and Mongolia flooding the global market with cheaper-to-produce soda ash.
WE Soda workers, joined by their families, attend a union meeting Nov. 12 in Rock Springs. (Dustin Bleizeffer/WyoFile)
The proposition of a state grant to help Wyoming operators compete in the tightening global market, local elected officials have said, is enticing but confounding due to the fact WE Soda’s job cuts are going to devastate the economy in southwest Wyoming.
“People won’t be able to pay their taxes, houses will go up for sale because people are going to lose their jobs,” Mayor-elect Stan Blake told WyoFile. “We have to help the miners who live in Green River.”
Gordon commended WE Soda for “working to get out in front of these major shifts.” But, he said, “the state needs to better understand how to efficiently and effectively support the industry, workers, families, and ancillary businesses that Sweetwater County depends on. State support through the WEA’s ongoing work in this area is vital.”
