(OPINION) The Mayor’s Minute with Patrick Collins (11/20/26)
by

CHEYENNE, Wyo. — This week saw two employees reach the 25th anniversary of their employment with the City. Captain Kevin Reddy has served as a firefighter, and I was able to join his team’s training at the Public Safety Center to present him with a plaque and thank him for his service to our community. It was interesting and gratifying to see the training the team was receiving to help them use tools to detect air quality contaminants. Having two crews and his wife on hand to help congratulate Captain Reddy made it even more special.
Vicki Nemecek joined the city 25 years ago after retiring from the Air Force. She started as the secretary for the Public Works Department and today is the director, running the whole division. I loved her story about moving to Iowa with her husband and after a few weeks deciding they missed Cheyenne and then moving back. We are blessed to have dedicated employees who choose to continue serving our residents. Thank you, Kevin and Vicki!
I had an emotional experience this week where I joined a group of 20 high school seniors for a presentation from the Jae Foundation. Executive Director Jansen Curry introduced us to the life of Jae Bing who was an amazing young man, loved by everyone who knew him, and unfortunately died by suicide at 27.
His best friend, Jason Vickrey, started the foundation to help educate students about how they can help with suicide prevention by encouraging people to slow down and check in with friends to have an honest conversation about their mental health. They call it a boot-check because the foundation gives high school seniors a free pair of cowboy boots as a visual reminder of the mission. It was emotional to hear Jae’s story and the story of loss among the students. It hit home to me as I have lost two friends in the past year to suicide. Think about doing boot-checks with your friends and loved ones this holiday season.
Bison Beverage celebrated its 100th anniversary this week. The company was started by Beryl Yarter-Lewis as Cheyenne Beverage in 1926. A woman-owned business 100 years ago was very unusual. Today, the fourth generation of the Lewis family, Todd and Brittney, are running the company and have expanded it to serve 18 Wyoming counties. I was honored to attend and speak about the amazing way they have given back to our community. A family has to do a lot right to make their family business successful for 100 years — it says a lot about the Lewis clan. Congratulations!
Judy and I attended the CFD 35th Annual Volunteer Appreciation Dinner. The nine Frontier Days committees recognize their top volunteers and awarded the volunteer of the year for each committee. Hearing what the volunteers did to warrant nomination was impressive. Watching the emotions of the winners and their committee supporters showed how important these awards are to the CFD family. I appreciate the effort of the huge number of volunteers who have made Cheyenne Frontier Days happen for more than 130 years now.
The MPO held an open house to get feedback on a proposal to extend Parsley Boulevard and Wallick Road. With all the development happening in town, growing our transportation system will improve our road network and reduce traffic impacts. I was impressed with the turnout, and I appreciated seeing Reps. Geringer and Styvar in attendance, joining Councilman Segrave. We have great partners with WYDOT, LEADS, the MPO, Laramie County and the city, along with companies developing in Cheyenne. The resources of this collaboration make successfully improving our transportation system possible.
The Cheyenne National Cemetery has a new director, William Reel. The cemetery opened in 2020 and serves anyone who has served in our military with a free place for the veteran and spouse to be buried. William and his family chose to move to Cheyenne and serve our veterans. The National Cemetery is a beautiful place, and I would encourage any veteran with questions to call Will at
307-778-7316
.
Creating the needed housing for our expanding workforce is a challenge I know many in our community are working to solve. I joined Councilmen Segrave and Laybourn for a tour of Hinterland Remote Housing Vehicles to see one option that can help house temporary workers. Bish’s RV center in Cheyenne hosted a tour of self-contained trailers Hinterland builds to meet workforce challenges. I was impressed by the quality of the units and the way they looked. I learned about how they have built a large workforce center in Abilene, Texas. It looks like a modern trailer park.
I know siting workforce housing centers is going to be a challenge, but touring the units and seeing an existing center made me feel like it might be possible. Finding housing for our temporary workforce, especially the folks constructing industrial developments, can reduce demand for our permanent housing units and I hope it can stabilize or reduce housing prices and improve availability.
I have a great group of directors that run the different divisions of city government. Two members of that team — City Treasurer Robin Lockman and Police Chief Mark Francisco — have recently let me know that they are going to retire and it is going to be so hard to see them go. Robin is responsible for the finances of the City and has done a magnificent job managing those resources. Her budget book is a thing of beauty and a tool I use all the time. Chief Francisco has done an amazing job leading our police department. I so appreciate how he has built the department’s team and culture. Additional evidence of their leadership is their succession plan and how they have trained their replacements. Chief Francisco has spent the past five years helping Captain David Janes prepare to become the next police chief. Robin has spent the past few years preparing Sarah Crone to become the next city treasurer. They both gave me their highest recommendation, and it is my intention to recommend them to City Council for confirmation when Robin leaves in August and Mark in April. They will spend the remaining months working together and preparing for the transition.
Thursday is Thanksgiving Day and it is my favorite day of the year. I love spending quality time with family, eating a special meal and being grateful for all the blessings we have. I hope you have a great holiday and ask you to remember the military members who are away from home making our celebration possible. We will also have first responders in fire stations, the police station, EMTs manning their ambulances and healthcare workers staffing the hospitals. I’m grateful for them and everyone who is working during the holiday to support our community.
