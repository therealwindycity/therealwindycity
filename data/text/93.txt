Sublette County attorney warned commissioners that targeting billionaire Ricketts’ resort is legally ‘dangerous’
by
Mike Koshmrl
,
WyoFile
After being told by their attorney that singling out billionaire Joe Ricketts puts them on legally shaky footing, Sublette County commissioners are moving ahead with plans to block the native Nebraskan’s effort to transform the Bondurant area by building a grocery store-sized luxury resort.
Meeting in Pinedale on Tuesday, commissioners decided in a 3-2 vote to invalidate a building permit that flew under the radar and
was approved by Sublette County planners
in September. The five-person board of commissioners also scheduled a Feb. 9 hearing in
pursuit of undoing current resort zoning
on the TD Ameritrade founder’s ranchland along Upper Hoback River Road.
Weeks before, Sublette County Attorney Clayton Melinkovich warned commissioners in an email that they were violating a “foundational” tenet of governance to “treat similarly situated people the same.” He quoted from the Declaration of Independence, 5th and 14th Amendments of the U. S. Constitution and from the Wyoming Constitution in support of his point.
“I witnessed two instances yesterday where Sublette County, through its [board of commissioners], made decisions that treated similarly situated people differently,” Melinkovich wrote to commissioners in
an Oct. 7 email
acquired by WyoFile.
Sublette County Attorney Clayton Melinkovich stands outside the Sublette County Justice Building on August 21, 2025 in Pinedale, Wyoming. (Erin Burk/WyoFile)
In one example, Melinkovich pointed toward the commissioners’ action to rezone Ricketts’ ranch. Commissioners reasoned that two years had lapsed since his resort was approved without “substantial development” — a condition in the
county’s zoning and development regulations
that is supposed to invalidate the zoning.
Melinkovich highlighted commissioners’ inconsistent treatment: “Sublette County has deliberately chosen to not initiate termination proceedings regarding two zone changes where the two-years’ time requirement had [also] lapsed,” he wrote, citing the Mountain Meadow and
Moyes family subdivisions
.
Melinkovich’s email to commissioners would normally be a non-public document because of attorney-client privilege — there’s a disclaimer at the bottom saying so. But because they forwarded it to each other, it was acquired by a citizen through a Wyoming Public Records Act request, and then shared with a representative for Ricketts and in turn WyoFile.
TD Ameritrade founder Joe Ricketts addresses Sublette County residents in May 2023. (Mike Koshmrl/WyoFile)
Melinkovich declined to comment on the email. In it, he told commissioners “in my legal opinion” that Sublette County walks “a very dangerous path when you step outside of your authority and disregard the foundational law of the land.”
He also told commissioners he has their back.
“Can the County succeed in terminat[ing] the zone change? Yes, it is possible,” he wrote. “Will my office vigorously defend your decision in the event that the [commission] terminates the zone? Yes, we certainly will. Does the County lose its credibility when it treats similarly situated individuals differently? Yes, full stop.”
Ricketts, whose family owns the Chicago Cubs, has been
trying for more than five years
to develop picturesque Bondurant, home to roughly 100 people in the Hoback Basin half way between Jackson and Pinedale. Although his efforts to overhaul Upper Hoback River Road have so far fallen short, nearby he’s been on a development tear.
North of Bondurant, Ricketts purchased in 2024 the 33-acre Safari Club International property along Granite Creek, where he
plans to expand and open
a guest ranch, dubbed The Hawken. The billionaire also purchased the Timberline Ranch near Merna, formerly occupied by
hunting influencers brought down for poaching
. And he bought the
embattled Renegade subdivision
up Greys River Road, an inholding he
tried and failed to trade to the Forest Service
. The property is now subject of a civil lawsuit
alleging he was defrauded out of tens of millions
of dollars.
In Sublette County, Lynn Bernard, Dave Stephens and Doug Vickrey have been the three commissioners voting to terminate Ricketts’ resort zoning. Bernard could not be reached immediately, and Stephens declined an interview. Vickrey told WyoFile that he’s doing the best he can to represent the community.
Sublette County Commissioner Doug Vickrey at an October 2022 meeting. (Mike Koshmrl/WyoFile)
“I looked around yesterday, and I saw a house full of folks concerned about what’s happening,” Vickrey said Wednesday morning. “I didn’t see many supporters for Mr. Ricketts. Mr. Ricketts’ entourage consisted of his lawyers.”
“Mr. Ricketts could have been a wonderful addition to the Bondurant community,” he added. “He chose not to do so, and why? I have no idea.”
A commercial building permit for a resort Ricketts dubbed “the Confluence” spurred the current dustup. A larger resort was under construction in 2024, but
Ricketts halted the project
after commissioners
declined to waive wildlife-friendly construction stipulations
that drove up the price tag.
Commissioners invalidated the permit for the Confluence on Tuesday in response to
an appeal
from neighbors Dan and Yvonne Bailey, Pat Burroughs, Marilyn Filkins, Tom Filkins, DJ Kominsky, Lisi Krall and Mark Prus. The now-stymied development was for a three-level, 47,000-square-foot resort that included lodging, a restaurant and spa, with an additional 47,000-square-foot underground parking garage.
During the meeting, Jackson attorney John Graham argued that Ricketts had achieved “substantial development” during his first attempt at building the resort, and he pointed toward millions of dollars in related investments. A
motion to intervene
in the neighbors’ appeal included a June 2023 letter from former Sublette County Planner Dennis Fornstrom that served as “documentation of substantial development.”
A detail showing the footprint of a 20-room guest lodge as part of a landscape plan submitted July 20, 2026, to Sublette County Planning and Zoning for a new development on Joe Ricketts’ property along the Upper Hoback River Road in Bondurant. (Courtesy)
“Jackson Fork [Ranch] relied on that representation,” Graham told commissioners. “Every step of the way, we were relying on Sublette County’s word, and Sublette County’s word is consistent with the regulations. Regulations don’t require vertical building; they require investment.”
Jackson attorney Leah Schwartz, representing the neighbors, encouraged commissioners Tuesday to reverse the resort zoning in addition to invalidating the building permit. They declined, instead setting a contested case hearing for Feb. 9, 2027. Another Jackson attorney, Justin Daraie, will preside over that hearing.
By that time, there will be changes in the composition of the Sublette County Board of Commissioners. Newcomers Andy Nelson and Robin Schamber unseated Vickrey and Commissioner Mack Bradley in the August
primary election
. Vickrey told WyoFile he has no inclinations as to how the new commission will decide the rezoning dispute.
“They’ll look at the information that’s there, and they’ll do the best they can to make the decision they feel is just,” Vickrey said. “It’s going to be an interesting ending to this saga.”
