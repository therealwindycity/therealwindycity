Man arrested for reportedly destroying Cheyenne hotel room, shattering window
CHEYENNE, Wyo. — Cheyenne police are charging a man with destroying a local hotel room, breaking windows, mirrors and walls and causing thousands of dollars of damage. Because the man reportedly bloodied his hands in the process, police said the hotel also had to pay for biohazard cleanup.
Mauro Albarran, born in 2004, is being charged with felony property destruction and misdemeanor interference. His felony charge carries a maximum potential penalty of 10 years in prison. He is considered innocent until pleading or being found guilty.
Police said that, shortly before midnight on Nov. 13, they were dispatched to the
My Place hotel
on Lincolnway to calls of a physical disturbance regarding a male hotel guest. The callers reportedly warned police that the man was aggressive and destroying his hotel room.
Upon arrival, they noticed a second-story window facing the parking lot was shattered and the glass was littering parts of the sidewalk. When they entered, staff told them that the man in that second-floor room was in town for work and that he had a knife in the room.
Police went to the room and tried to speak to the man, identified by hotel staff as Albarran, from the doorway. Officers noticed Albarran’s hands were bloody and asked him to show them. He was then physically removed from the room and cuffed in the hallway. He reportedly fought back multiple times while being escorted to a patrol car, pulling and jumping away from officers. He refused until he had to be placed on the ground for officer safety, reportedly kicking his legs after he was brought down.
The hotel room, meanwhile, was reportedly destroyed. The exterior window and bathroom mirror were both shattered, the shower curtain rod was ripped out of the wall, the bathroom wall was damaged and a cabinet door was ripped off its hinges. Across the rest of the hotel room, furniture and the walls and floor were covered in blood, the affidavit states.
Police estimate that the cost of replacing the window is between $500 and $2,600. It could cost an additional $40–$170 to install it. The damage to the bathroom could cost an extra few hundred dollars. Biohazard cleaning for all of the blood could cost upwards of $6,000.
Now Albarran awaits a preliminary hearing next week, where a Laramie County Circuit Court judge will determine if his case should be taken up by a higher court. He has been issued a $5,000 cash bond in the meantime.
