Rebecca Bextel, former governor candidate known for Checkgate, arrested for suspected drunk driving
Police say they found empty mini bottles of vodka in the SUV driven by the conservative activist when they stopped her Thursday night.
by
Wyofile
11 hours ago
November 23, 2026

Rebecca Bextel, a conservative activist who unsuccessfully ran for governor, was arrested Thursday night in Jackson on suspicion of driving drunk, police there said.
Bextel faces a driving under the influence charge along with three traffic citations including driving with an open container of alcohol, police told WyoFile. It was not immediately clear when she would make her first court appearance.
She did not respond to a text message seeking comment Friday morning.
After being stopped Thursday evening, Bextel told one Jackson police officer she’d consumed two shots of vodka before driving, and a second officer found a mini Tito’s Vodka bottle in the driver’s side door and two others in the front center console, according to her arrest affidavit, which Jackson Police Lt. Russ Ruschill read to WyoFile. Two of the bottles were empty, and a third had a small amount of liquid in it, the affidavit said.
“I would characterize this as a fairly common DUI for Jackson,” he said. “There’s nothing really outstanding about it from law enforcement’s perspective. It does support our mission of public safety by getting drunk drivers off the road.”
Jackson Police Officer Riley Williams stopped Bextel after police received a report of a possible drunk driver at 9:42 p.m. Thursday. Williams spotted an SUV matching the description traveling east on West Broadway near the intersection of Flat Creek Drive, the affidavit states.
In that report, Williams describes the SUV crossing into the oncoming lane of traffic before merging back into the correct lane. He turned on his lights and then sirens, and the SUV continued driving east and failed to completely stop at the intersection of Willow Street. Police say the SUV almost hit a truck driving south through the intersection at Willow Street before coming to a complete stop.
Constitution Party candidate Rebecca Bextel laughs after taking a jab at Eric Barlow during the Wyoming PBS gubernatorial debate on Wednesday, Oct. 14, 2026, in Riverton. (Carl Cote/County 10)
A second officer, Kenton Kramer, also arrived at the scene, and both officers reported smelling alcohol on Bextel’s breath and hearing her slur her speech, according to the affidavit. Bextel agreed to perform field sobriety tests.
“During all three batteries of tests, the officer observed signs of impairment,” Ruschill said.
Officers performed a breath test at the scene, which indicated Bextel had a blood alcohol concentration of .201%, according to the affidavit. In Wyoming, a person with a blood alcohol concentration of .08% is presumed too drunk to drive.
After officers took Bextel to jail, they attempted to obtain the results from a breath test there, but were unable to get a sufficient sample, according to the affidavit. She “refused to do another chemical breath test and refused the options of blood or urine,” Ruschill said. Officers obtained a DUI search warrant, a nurse at the local hospital took a blood sample around midnight.
Ruschill said portable tests at the scene can be affected by the cold, and it would take four to six weeks before results from the blood sample come back.
“So I wouldn’t put any credence in the .201 until we got the result back from the blood test, but there was definitely alcohol on board,” he said.
Bextel
describes herself
as “the public face” of opposing Teton County’s mitigation fees designed to fund affordable housing. In 2025, she
unsuccessfully ran
for chairman of the Wyoming Republican Party.
Rebecca Bextel testifies during the first meeting of the House Special Investigative Committee on Thursday, Feb. 26, 2026, at the Wyoming Capitol in Cheyenne. (Mike Vanata for WyoFile)
She became more well known in February when she
handed out campaign checks
to Wyoming lawmakers on the House floor. The incident, known as Checkgate, dominated the 2026 legislative session and sparked multiple investigations, including an ongoing one by the Laramie County Sheriff’s Office. She denied any wrongdoing, and a legislative investigation determined the check passing did not amount to bribery. The panel also voted unanimously to adopt a report that admonished Bextel’s conduct.
Bextel earned roughly 17% of the vote in the general election — well short of Republican Eric Barlow, though enough to elevate the Constitution Party
to major status
.
In May,
Bextel announced
she would leave the Republican party to run as a third-party candidate for governor if Republican voters nominated Gillette Sen. Eric Barlow in the primary election. She is now
facing multiple complaints
filed with the Wyoming Secretary of State’s office regarding her campaign finance reports.
WyoFile staff writer Maggie Mullen contributed to this report.
