Temporary laborer held on $500K bond after reportedly accidentally shooting coworker

CHEYENNE, Wyo. — A man living in an RV in Cheyenne while he and his son perform roofing work in town is being charged with voluntary attempted manslaughter after reportedly accidentally shooting a coworker with another coworker’s gun. He is being held on a $500,000 bond and has a preliminary hearing scheduled for next week.
Laramie County Sheriff’s Office deputies wrote in the criminal affidavit for Javier Ruiz, born in 1985, that law enforcement first learned of suspected wrongdoing after a man appeared at the Cheyenne Regional Medical Center with a gunshot wound in the abdomen the evening of Nov. 13.
The man was reportedly conscious, but refused to cooperate with medical staff in identifying himself. His condition soon took a turn for the worse and he had to be taken in for emergency surgery. He survived but remained in the hospital, heavily sedated.
The man who took him to the hospital was reportedly uncooperative at first as well, but eventually told Cheyenne Police Department officers that the incident happened at an RV park off of College Drive. The area being in unincorporated Laramie County, the case was turned over to the sheriff’s office.
Deputies traveled to the park and made contact with Ruiz and another man. Ruiz reportedly told deputies that he and his son, the man who brought the injured person to the hospital, were living in Cheyenne temporarily to do roofing work. The father–son duo had just picked up two other workers from Colorado a few days earlier and were letting them stay in their RV, he said.
That evening, they cooked ribs and reportedly got drunk. Another coworker had appeared, Ruiz said, and left a handgun on Ruiz’s bed. Deputies were told that earlier in the night, Ruiz inspected the gun and noted that it was missing its magazine, leading him to believe that it was unloaded.
Later, when everyone was getting ready for bed, Ruiz said he was messing around with the firearm. Deputies suspect that Ruiz, still thinking it was unloaded, aimed it at one of his coworkers and pulled the trigger. The gun fired and the man was struck.
Court documents state that Ruiz said there was no arguments or incidents that led to the firing; it was an accident. The statement was corroborated by Ruiz’s son and the other witness, the affidavit states. They had a good evening, they all said, and were winding down for the night when they heard a gunshot.
Because he reportedly seriously injured another man, Ruiz is charged with felony attempted manslaughter. Deputies said that, even if he believed the gun was not loaded, Ruiz is suspected of deliberately pointing it at another person and pulling the trigger. The charge carries a potential maximum penalty of 20 years in prison.
Ruiz is presumed innocent until convicted in a court of law. He will appear for a preliminary hearing in Laramie County Circuit Court early next week.
The man that deputies said Ruiz shot has regained full consciousness and is medically stable. He is expected to survive.
