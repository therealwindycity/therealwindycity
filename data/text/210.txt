CHEYENNE, Wyo. — It is Thanksgiving evening as I write this week’s Minute – football on TV, belly full of turkey, and feeling thankful for all the blessings in our lives. This is the first year in decades I have not cooked Thanksgiving dinner. Judy and I met my sister, Maureen, and her husband, Gordon, from Denver halfway for dinner. It was different having someone serve you dinner, but not having to clean up the kitchen was something I could get used to. Judy and I hope everyone had a peaceful day filled with family, friends, and good food!
I was the Military Affairs Committee chairman in 2015 when we met with the Air Force to discuss ways the community could partner with the military. One project that came out of that effort was the Enhanced Use Lease (EUL) that would develop housing and 50 acres of new commercial development on land owned by the Air Force, but outside the security fence. You would know the area as the current park-and-ride for Frontier Days off Happy Jack Road. We met with the Wyoming Business Council (WBC) this week to discuss the grant and loan they made to support the sewer infrastructure for the project. The grant expires at the end of the year, and we updated the WBC on the lease negotiations between the private developer and Air Force. The project will see 350 housing units built in the first phase with new restaurants and shopping on the rest. The good news is after a decade of negotiations, it appears the lease of the property will happen very soon. I appreciate the WBC’s patience as we worked through the various processes.
The Wyoming State Fire Academy graduated 12 new firefighters, including five from the Cheyenne area this week. The Academy was started in 2022 by Cheyenne Fire Rescue when we decided to train our own recruits. It has grown from Cheyenne, Laramie, and Sheridan attending the first class to adding Rawlins, Rock Springs, Campbell County, and the Wyoming Air Guard in this class. Past classes have included the departments from Casper and Scottsbluff. It is much less expensive than sending recruits out of state, with each department sending instructors to share the burden of training. Please welcome Jeff Yepsen, Cory Kerr, Riley Holzhausen, and Andrew Yao as the newest members of Cheyenne’s department. Mason Munford, also from Cheyenne, was sworn in as the newest member of the Wyoming Air Guard fire department. With the academy growing, it has made me think about the future need to build a new facility to support this vital training effort. I’m proud of the statewide collaboration.
Judy and I were excited to attend the ribbon cutting of Dunkin’. I figured a donut and a good cup of coffee would make it a great morning. Having Central High School’s band and cheerleaders from East and South high schools join the 100 people in line gave the morning lots of energy and excitement. We had a blast, and the donuts made the morning perfect.
Our Community Recreation & Events (CRE) department joined Visit Cheyenne and Downtown First to make last Saturday evening special. Downtown First held a winter market on 17th Street with lots of fun vendors. Visit Cheyenne hosted the Old West Holiday where folks mounted on horseback placed wreaths on the 28 Capitol Avenue bronzes while Nathaniel narrated the meaning behind each bronze. CRE decorated the depot and plaza for the holiday, and Santa & Mrs. Claus took pictures with excited kids (including Judy and I!). I loved the station where kids could write letters to Santa and I heard he just might write back. The highlight was the lighting of the Christmas tree. So many people worked so hard to make this event special. Thank you, everyone!
Cheyenne’s Zonta Club works each year to recognize the 16 days of Activism Against Gender-Based Violence. We met this week to sign a proclamation and discuss their efforts to educate our community on their efforts to curb gender-based violence. The 16 days between International Day to Eliminate Violence Against Women on Nov. 25 and International Human Rights Day on Dec. 10 are recognized by thousands of communities. This worldwide problem causes so much mental and physical trauma and costs billions annually in medical expenses, police and court costs, shelter, foster care, sick leave, and absenteeism. I appreciate all that is being done to combat this problem, and I hope someday we won’t have to recognize these 16 days anymore.
During Covid, the Governing Body had to find new ways to meet and for the public to communicate with their representatives. City Council chose to use Zoom for participation and to also stream the meetings on YouTube. Once we started meeting in person again, we continued to allow remote public comment on Zoom. I believed giving the public more options to participate in our meetings was good policy. Unfortunately, we have one individual who uses the Zoom testimony option to comment frivolously on every item. While his participation is within our current rules, adding 45 minutes to an hour to every meeting is frustrating to members of the Governing Body and residents who come to the meetings in person. We have had folks who have waited to testify on issues important to them leave out of exasperation before their opportunity to speak. We give everyone three minutes on a topic to share their thoughts, but when one person testifies 20 times a meeting – and that is not unusual – it adds an hour to our meetings. I personally love when people testify at our meetings, even when their opinion is contrary to my perspective. Most people come ready to speak, share valid observations, and are succinct. They add to the meeting. I am concerned this incessant speaking will cause the Governing Body to change our rules to limit remote testimony or eliminate it. I have tried to get the individual to speak with me about my concerns, but he won’t meet. It’s frustrating that one person could ruin it for a whole community.
Two interesting things from our latest City Council meeting. First, we approved an agreement with WYDOT where they share their buying power for epoxy striping to make our 5th Penny sales tax dollars go further. I appreciate their willingness to work with our team. Second, we approved a final plat for 130 housing units at the intersection of Ridge Road and Holland Court. This area was previously planned to be almost 250 apartment units, but a zoning protest petition caused it to fail. The developer has redesigned the project with less density, and while I am still disappointed the first design failed, I am happy to see the infill area developed to meet our growing housing needs.
Hope to see you at the Christmas Parade on Saturday! The weather should be perfect, and so many people have worked all year to make it epic.
