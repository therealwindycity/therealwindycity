Ernestine Alice Chandler
Ernestine Alice Chandler:
1930 – 2026
Ernestine “Ernie” Alice Chandler, beloved wife, sister and aunt died on November 11, 2026, at the age of 96. She was born in Fort Collins, Colorado, to Leopoldo, Sr. and Natividad (Chavez) Garcia. She graduated from St. Mary’s High School in 1948.
Ernestine met her husband Allen in Cheyenne and they married on August 16, 1952. As a homemaker, she devoted herself to caring for her parents and family members. She enjoyed volunteering at St. Joseph’s Food Pantry and worked parttime at the Elks Lodge. She loved to read and watch Jeopardy. Gardening, taking walks, and walking on her treadmill kept her young and healthy into her 90s. She stopped bike riding in her 80s.
She was a devoted wife, whose life was centered around God, family and friends. Ernestine was a devout Catholic and a member of St. Joseph’s. Her faith was the foundation of her life. She found comfort and strength in her relationship with God. Her generosity was quiet, sincere, and she never hesitated to lend a hand to anyone in need.
Ernestine was preceded in death by her husband, Allen; her parents; brothers, Leo and Rudy; nephews, Jimmy Garcia, Christopher McQuade; and niece, Mary Chandler. She is survived by her brother, George (Sharon); sisters-in-law, Doris Garcia and Daisy Garcia. Also surviving Ernestine are her nieces: Cathy, Suzi, Meri, Lori, Christine, Elizabeth, Marina, Susan, Judy, Janet and Chrissy; and nephews: Theodore, Scott, Allen, Steven and Boris and all their families.
In lieu of flowers, donations may be made to St. Joseph’s Food Pantry or the donor’s choice. Viewing will be held November 19 from 1 to 4 p.m. followed by a vigil at 5 p.m. at Wiederspahn Radomsky Chapel. A Funeral Liturgy will be held on Friday, November 20 at 11 a.m. at St. Joseph’s. Interment will follow at Olivet Cemetery. Expressions of care can be sent to www.wrcfuneral.com.
Marion Agnes Hixon:
1924 – 2026
Marion HIxon, age 102, passed away peacefully with family by her side at Primrose in Cheyenne, Wyoming, on Saturday, November 14, 2026.
Marion was born July 31, 1924, to Sylvester and Agnes (Walker) Boylan of Salem, New Hampshire.
She married Harry Hixon April 4, 1948, and they began raising their family in Mellette, South Dakota. They farmed in the Turton and Chelsea areas before moving to the rural Aberdeen area. Marion sold World Book encyclopedias and worked in retail in Aberdeen and Sioux Falls, South Dakota. She was active in her Catholic churches, St. Vincent De Paul, and Altar Society. Harry and Marion retired to Florida in 1990 and they enjoyed traveling to visit family. Marion also enjoyed crafts and reading and made the best fried chicken and brownies.
She is survived by her children: Harry Hixon III and his wife, Rene, Candyce Nesheim; Mary Moyer and her husband, Mike; and Camille Van Voorst and her husband, Brad; daughters-in-law, Gail Hixon and Margaret Hixon; grandchildren: Amanda, Jake, Nathan, Robert, Denise, David, Rebecca, Harris, Matt, Shawna, Zach and Aaron; 17 great-grandchildren; and three great-great-grandchildren; and many other relatives and friends.
Marion was preceded in death by her husband, Harry; her parents; sons, Michael and David; son-in-law, Odean Nesheim; grandson, Patrick Hixon; granddaughter-in-law, Jeanne Hixon; and nine siblings.
Services to be announced at a later date.
Leola Jackson:
1931 – 2026
Leola Jackson, 95, of Cheyenne, Wyoming, passed away on November 10, 2026, leaving behind a beautiful legacy of faith, love, strength, and perseverance.
Born September 10, 1931, in Centreville, Mississippi, to Idora and Joseph James, a World War II veteran, Leola grew up knowing the meaning of hard work and sacrifice. While living in Mississippi, she met and married the love of her life, David Jackson Sr. In 1952, they moved to Cheyenne with the railroad, where they built a life together and raised 11 children.
She was preceded in death by her parents, Joseph and Idora James; her beloved husband, David Jackson Sr.; and her daughter, Joanne Merritt.
Services will be held at Second Baptist Church on Tuesday, November 24, 2026, at 10 a.m.
Delma Martinez:
1953 – 2026
It is with heavy hearts that we share the passing of Helen Delma Martinez, a beloved mother, grandmother, great-grandmother, sister, aunt, cousin, and dear friend who was truly one of a kind.
Delma was born on December 26, 1953, in Cheyenne, Wyoming, and passed away on November 13, 2026, at the age of 72, surrounded by the family she loved so dearly.
Delma was stubborn, fiery, fiercely independent, and never one to sugarcoat the truth. You always knew where you stood with her. She had a strong personality, a heart just as big, and a way of leaving an impression on everyone she met.
Above all else, Delma loved her family more than anything. Her love was deep and unconditional, and there was never any doubt about how much her family meant to her. She was always thinking of others and seemed to have a little something for everyone. Whether it was some unexpected treasure she had picked up because it reminded her of you or something she thought you might need, you could almost always count on leaving a visit with Delma with something in your hands. And whatever the gift might have been, it always had a touch of style, because that was one thing Delma never lacked. It was one of the many ways she showed how much she cared.
She loved hard, spoke her mind, and lived life on her own terms. She had a mind of her own and a spirit that couldn’t be changed, but everything about her was genuine, and that’s what made her so special. Her spirit was unforgettable, and there will never be another quite like her.
Delma is survived by her daughter, Mercedes Martinez (Brandon); her granddaughters: Destanie Dettmann (Cody), Savanna Worthington (Cody), and Mya Martinez; and her great-grandchildren: Brailee, Haislee, Arbor, and Brewer. She is also survived by her siblings: Jerry Roybal, Dickie (Margie) Roybal, Linda (Martin) Abeyta, Cathy Zuniga, and Emily (Jessie) Llanez; as well as many nieces, nephews, and other relatives.
She was preceded in death by her beloved son, Lucas Martinez; her parents, Jose and Mercedes Roybal; her brothers: Adam, Rocky, Valentine, Isadore, Jimmy, and Joe; and her sisters, Delores Torres and Mary (Hopie) Macias.
While our hearts are broken, we find comfort in the memories, the laughter, and even those moments when her stubbornness got the best of us. Those are the things that made Delma who she was, and those are the things we will hold close forever.
She was deeply loved, and she will be missed more than words can ever express.
Celebration of Life
A Celebration of Life honoring Delma will be held on Saturday, November 21, 2026, at 10 a.m. at Restoration Fellowship INT’L Church Assemblies of God, located at 522 Capitol Ave, Cheyenne, WY 82007, with a reception to follow.
In lieu of flowers, please consider making a donation to Robbie’s House, a nonprofit organization dedicated to supporting individuals living with mental illness and helping others navigate mental health challenges through compassion, connection, and community.
https://www.robbieshouselaramie.org/

Shirley Mae Robinson:
1948 – 2026
It is with heavy hearts and deep love, we announce the passing of Shirley M. Robinson, who passed away peacefully at Davis Hospice Center on November 4, 2026, at the age of 78.
Born on January 30, 1948, in Cheyenne, Wyoming, to Samuel and Pearl Reid. Shirley was a beloved mother, grandmother, sister, aunt, and friend whose life was filled with love, strength, kindness, and devotion to her faith and family. Throughout her life, she demonstrated grace, determination, and a generous spirit. She touched the lives of many through her compassion, laughter, and unwavering love.
Shirley had a special love for music and found great joy in singing and playing the piano and organ at numerous churches throughout Cheyenne. Her music was more than a gift; it was an expression of her faith and love for the lord. Through every note she played, she shared her heart, uplifted spirits, and helped bring joy and inspiration to all. Her faith and love of music will forever be cherished by those who had the pleasure of hearing her play. The family would like to thank Second Baptist Church, Holy Temple Church of God in Christ, Allen Chapel AME Church, Unity Missionary Baptist Church, New Hope Church of God in Christ, Salvation Army, and Harvest Time Christian Fellowship for their love and support of Shirley over the years.
Shirley is survived by sons, Michael Robinson and Paul (Brandi) Robinson; granddaughters: Kayla Lesh, Tasarra and Eden Robinson; siblings: Patricia Brown, Vera Jones, and James Reid; and numerous nieces and nephews.
She was preceded in death by her parents, Samuel and Pearl Reid; sister, Evelyn Reid; brother-in-law, Arbia Jones; and niece, Elizabeth Brown.
Though our hearts are saddened by her passing, we find comfort in knowing that Shirley’s love, faith, music, and beautiful memories will live on in all who knew her.
A life well lived, a song well played, and a love never forgotten.
Rest peacefully, Shirley. May the heavenly choir welcome you home. Your song will forever live in our hearts.
No funeral services will be held at this time. In lieu of flowers, memorial donations may be made to Davis Hospice Center, who took special care of Shirley in her final weeks. The family is forever grateful for their compassion and care.
William Emmett Vernon:
1944 – 2026
William Emmett ‘Bill’ Vernon, aged 82, died at home near Cheyenne on November 14, 2026, his wife at his side and his dogs in his lap.
Bill was born April 20, 1944 in Seattle, Washington to parents Emmett Samuel Vernon and Delma Alma Learned. His young childhood was spent running the woods and hunting and fishing with his dad.
When Bill was a preteen, the family relocated to southern California, where his father worked for Walter Knott at Knott’s Berry Farm in Buena Park, California. Bill’s favorite memory of that time was frequently walking the park with Mr. Knott early in the morning before opening time.
Later, the family moved again, this time to Calico Ghost Town, an 1800s silver mining town-turned tourist attraction near Barstow, California, where the family lived and worked, again for Walter Knott, as proprietors of the General Store.
In Calico, Bill ran the horse and burro rides, worked on the maintenance crew, and did stunts, robbing the train and performing in shootouts in the street. He became an excellent horseman, working now and then for ranches in the area. He rodeoed until he separated his sternum in one wreck and dislocated a hip in another.
Early on, he quit high school, packed up, and moved to Utah where he worked ranches in the southwest corner of the state. His dad called one day to tell him the school counselor was looking for him, saying Bill couldn’t quit school until he was 16. Bill called the school. When the counselor told him the same, Bill said, “I’m in Utah. Come and get me.” No one came, but he eventually returned to California to continue working in Calico, and later for the Southern Pacific Railroad as a switchman/brakeman. He then worked at Fort Irwin, near Barstow, operating heavy equipment.
In 1966, Bill volunteered for the draft and was inducted into the U. S. Army, completing basic training at Fort Ord, California, later stationed at Fort Derosier in Virginia.
Bill served in Vietnam in 1967 and 1968. He was wounded twice during the Tet Offensive of 1968, and was awarded the Bronze Star with ‘V’ device for valor, and the Purple Heart.
Following his service, Bill returned to Barstow and his job at Ft Irwin. In 1969, he married Linda and the two moved to Trinity County in Northern California where Bill worked logging and at a small sawmill. He also became a licensed guide. The couple lived without electricity, eight miles from neighbors and eighty miles from a town of any size.
Not satisfied with the GED he earned in the military, Bill drove 24-miles on a one-lane switchback road, three nights a week, to tiny Hayfork, California, where he attended high school night courses to earn his diploma.
The couple returned to the desert, this time to El Centro, California where Bill worked for the Bureau of Land Management as a maintenance man, caring for a number of BLM campgrounds in the vast California Desert District. Working by day, he attended college at night, earning his AA degree in Physical Anthropology and his BA in Geography. He also served as a reserve police officer for the El Centro Police Department.
In 1977, Bill began his career as a Dept of Interior Bureau of Land Management(BLM) Ranger. He attended the Federal Law Enforcement Training Center at Glynco, Georgia and in 1978 he became one of the first 13 BLM Rangers to receive law enforcement delegation. He worked out of the El Centro Field Office, his patrol sector including the Imperial Sand Dunes a vast off-road vehicle playground, visited by thousands each year. In 1980, Bill was promoted to Chief Ranger. In 1983, he was selected for attendance at the FBI’s prestigious National Academy, where he earned a Master’s Degree in Public Administration from the University of Virginia.
In 1988, Bill reported for duty as a BLM Special Agent in Cheyenne, Wyoming, as such working several complex and high profile natural resource cases. In 1994, Bill was again promoted, this time to Special-Agent-In-Charge for Wyoming and Nebraska, continuing to serve at the Wyoming State Office in Cheyenne. Throughout his career with BLM, Bill also served as a wildland firefighter, fire boss, and security manager. He retired in December of 2000.
Bill was a proud Cheyenne Frontier Days Security Committee Volunteer and HEELS member for 30 years. He also spent 52 years as Santa Claus beginning as a terrified 25 year old, quickly inhabiting the role as time went on. He visited homes, schools, parties, banquets, and clubs, and was often chauffeured on Christmas Eve, making surprise visits, enthralling children and adults alike.
Bill was predeceased by his parents; his brother, Virgil; and a son, William. He leaves his wife of 57 years, Linda, of Cheyenne; his daughter, Kathleen; son-in-law, Will; and his two grandchildren, all of San Antonio, Texas; as well as his three beloved dogs: Lily, Ebenezer, and Riley.
As Bill wished, there will be no funeral service. As he served the public in life, he continues to serve today as a body donor, his remains being studied at the University of Colorado Medical school.
A military internment ceremony at the National Cemetery in Cheyenne will be planned at a later date.
In the interim and in lieu of flowers, please consider donating to the LCCC Rodeo Program or the animal shelter of your choice.
