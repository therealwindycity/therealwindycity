Wyoming’s average is down 17 cents to $3.72 per gallon, marking a drop of 58 cents from three weeks ago. AAA reported an 18-cent drop in the national average to $3.63, with
price-tracking service GasBuddy

Today’s average is down 74.1 cents from a month ago, but remains up 52.9 cents from a year ago, GasBuddy said. The national average price of diesel fell 19.2 cents over the last week to $5.80 per gallon.

Natrona County’s $3.42 average, down 23 cents, is the state’s second cheapest average, followed by Crook County’s $3.44, according to AAA.
Gasoline and diesel prices dropped in nearly every state last week, leading to the lowest national gas average since mid-March just ahead of Thanksgiving, said Patrick De Haan, head of petroleum analysis at GasBuddy.
“Our Thanksgiving survey found that 66% of Americans still plan to hit the road, even though 78% say gas costs more than it did last year and nearly one in five are cutting back on other holiday spending to cover fuel,” De Haan said. “The timing of this relief is welcome, and it now looks doubtful that we’ll surpass 2022’s Thanksgiving Day record of $3.558 per gallon.”
The national average could bottom out between $3.35 and $3.55 over the next few weeks, he added, with cheaper states offering $2.99 gas during Thanksgiving holiday travels.
“If the situation in the Middle East stays quiet or improves, these lower prices could stick around for some of the winter,” De Haan said.
Oil prices slid after a third straight weekly loss as supply outweighed another weekend of attacks in the Persian Gulf. The International Energy Agency expects a fourth-quarter surplus as Middle East exports recover.
“Higher Gulf exports should not be confused with a normalization of the oil market,” said Giovanni Staunovo, commodities analyst at UBS, adding that export flows still depend on U.S. military support and “the risk of renewed supply disruptions remains elevated.”
In early Monday trade, West Texas Intermediate crude was down $1.77 to $81.64 per barrel, well below last Monday’s $87.64. Brent crude fell $2.07 to $90.50 compared to $94.43 a week ago, GasBuddy reported.
U.S. commercial crude oil inventories rose by 3.4 million barrels for the week ending Nov. 13 and are about 3% below the five-year average for this time of year, according to the U.S. Energy Information Administration. Gasoline inventories rose by 5.2 million barrels, sitting about 3% above the five-year seasonal average, while distillate inventories rose by 1.6 million barrels.
