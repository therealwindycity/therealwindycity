Cheyenne PD, US Marshals make pair of arrests in south Cheyenne, prompting school response

CHEYENNE, Wyo. — Two arrests were successfully made during a police operation in south Cheyenne today. Due to a heavy police presence,
three neighborhood schools had to be ordered into a secure perimeter
.
At around 9 a.m. Thursday, Cheyenne police announced on Facebook that Goins Elementary, South High School and Johnson Junior High School,
which make up a larger school neighborhood in southwest Cheyenne
, were placed into secure perimeter. Secure perimeters, as opposed to lockdowns, allow classes to operate normally while prohibiting staff and students from going outside. While sparse on details, the morning announcement clarified that the decision was made due to police activity in the area and not because there was a threat to any of the schools.
Shortly before 3 p.m., the police announced that it successfully assisted in a U.S. Marshals Service Fugitive Task Force operation to serve two warrants in the neighborhood and there is no longer any police presence in the area.
The task force was first dispatched to the area to arrest 19-year-old Izaak Martinez, who officers believed was staying at an apartment on the 500 block of South Snyder Avenue. He was wanted for escape from the Cheyenne Transitional Center and reckless endangerment with a firearm.
Martinez was not found at the apartment, but officers did locate the apartment’s owner, 21-year-old Jaidah Carabajal, who had an outstanding warrant for failure to appear. She was taken into custody without incident.
Learning that Martinez was at a residence at West Jefferson Road, Officers obtained a search warrant and arrested him there at around 1 p.m.
Jaidah Carabajal (Cheyenne PD)
Izaak Martinez (Cheyenne PD)
The locations have been cleared and both individuals were booked into the Laramie County Jail on their outstanding warrants, Cheyenne police said. The U.S. Marshals Service will continue its investigation into Martinez and Carabajal as the lead agency.
Both suspects are presumed innocent until pleading or being found guilty to their charges.
In an unrelated incident Thursday morning, nearby Afflerbach Elementary School was also put on secure perimeter. Laramie County sheriffs said that the order was also to allow deputies space to make an arrest.
