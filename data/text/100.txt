Cheyenne agriculture reforms up for final reading by City Council tonight
CHEYENNE, Wyo. — Postponed from a previous week’s meeting, the Cheyenne City Council is set to take a vote tonight on an ordinance establishing Unified Development Code rules and definitions for agriculture. If approved, the updates would effectively allow agriculture as a land use in town.
The council is set to meet at 6 p.m. in the Council Chambers of the Municipal Building, located at 2101 O’Neil Ave. The public can also access the meeting remotely
via YouTube
or telephone.
Councilors have just a single ordinance on third reading tonight, and it’s been on their agenda for months. Following the city’s move to
wrap up county pocket annexations
earlier this year, a local urban farm and farmstand operation, originally operating in the more agriculture-friendly county, was slated for incorporation. Without rules allowing agriculture as a use, though, the business was doomed to fail if it was immediately annexed.
So the city postponed the annexation for months and
prompted planning staff to draft UDC amendments incorporating agriculture as a viable land use in certain zones
. The
annexation went through
, but councilors moved to postpone the vote on some of the rule changes another two weeks to consider a provision added later that would grandfather in legal nonconforming agricultural uses upon annexation.
Now it’s back for consideration in front of the council. If approved, it would establish standardized “Animal Unit” measurements to determine livestock density within city limits, regardless of live weight. Large livestock like horses and cattle would count as one AU, while small animals like chickens and rabbits only count as a fraction of an AU. Properties can house one AU per acre. If a property exceeds one AU per acre, it is considered a concentrated feeding operation, which requires a special permit.
Other changes establish different types of agricultural operations the city allows, including gardens and homemade foods, agritourism, general agriculture and the aforementioned concentrated feeding operations. Each of these comes with specific rules and rights for landowners.
The only change to the ordinance before the council tonight could be introduced as a substitute amendment. If passed alongside the ordinance, the substitute would allow agricultural operations annexed by the city to continue their operations under grandfathered status.
Also up for final consideration are agreements with the Wyoming Department of Transportation to fund concrete pavement rehabilitation at Campstool Way near Walmart and to perform epoxy striping around town. The council will also move to
discharge uncollectible debts from 2022
, adjust some city employees’ salaries,
approve two plats
,
approve two liquor license applications
and more. Other council action includes referring multiple items to the city council’s two committees for a closer look.
