(OPINION) Thankful thoughts from the Cap City newsroom

Happy Thanksgiving, Cheyenne! Garrett from Cap City News here. You’ve probably seen my byline if you’ve visited our site in the last five months, but you probably don’t know much about me. In light of the holiday season, I wanted to shed my journalistic objectivity for a bit and talk about what I’m thankful for.
To start, I’m thankful for the opportunity and thankful to you for reading.
I moved to Cheyenne on July 10, arriving with a BA of Journalism from the University of Wyoming in my hand and a twinkle in my eye. I didn’t know a lot about Cheyenne at the time, but I was excited to make this town my home as I prepared to take on my first job post-graduation.
I was uneasy at first — I’ve never moved to a town where I didn’t know a single soul before — but steeled my nerves. In just a few days, on July 13, I would get to cover a really exciting story:
The City of Cheyenne was preparing to annex 3,460 acres of land in Laramie County that Microsoft was planning to develop a data center campus on
. I had to be ready.
I’m thankful for the incredible amount of preparation I had through my education. I don’t feel like a fish out of water when I do my job. The University of Wyoming Communication & Journalism Department is a small but very qualified group of instructors. Classes with them instilled me with the confidence and know-how I needed to do this job right, which makes for better stories for the people of Cheyenne to read. I’m thankful that Wyoming has for so long prioritized quality education, supporting the university and giving young adults like me the opportunity to carve out my future at an affordable price. I was, thankfully, ready to handle that first assignment.
On the evening of July 13, I listened to hours of the most impassioned testimony I had ever heard in a public forum. My job is to be objective — and I always will be — but I’m thankful to live in a community with so many caring souls, both members of the public and our public servants. I’ve seen both tell people what they think and open their ear to others.
In those meetings, which are usually a little more easy-going than that particular one, I’m always thankful for how neighborly Cheyenne feels. I am from Wyoming; I grew up in Casper. There isn’t anywhere I’d rather be than here in the Cowboy State. In Cheyenne, the people are friendly, the traffic is light, and I’m never far from a creek to fly-fish in or a trail to hike on. If I need something from someone, I can just go up and say hello. I’m a bit of an awkward guy around people I don’t know well, so I appreciate all of the folks who have helped break the ice and worked with me on stories.
I’m thankful to my employers for giving me a chance four years ago, a chance that led me to stay in the state I love. I was 18, my dad had just died of cancer and my first year of college was completely derailed. I moved back home for two semesters, changed my major to journalism on a whim and applied for a summer internship with Oil City News in Casper, thinking that I’d get to make coffee runs for some real journalists. I was incredibly surprised when my editor, Klark Byrd, asked me to write an actual story. Then another. And another. I was also surprised when I was asked to intern again the next summer and when they offered me part-time work while I attended school in Laramie.
I now get to work for Cap City News, Oil City News’s sister site, with coworkers I love, with leadership I know believes in me and for communities I care deeply for. I’m so thankful for that. I’m so happy that I can work for you, Cheyenne.
During my final semester of university, I went on a study abroad trip to Chile. I lived in Santiago with an 81-year-old man, Salvador, and his huge family. Between them and my classmates at Universidad Andrés Bello, I learned that community is what you make it. Being embraced so openly by people I didn’t know and who were worlds different than me taught me that I could be friends with anyone.
I want to bring that spirit to Cheyenne. I hope you say hi and stop for a chat if you recognize me while I’m out and about. Another reason Wyoming is great is that I see people I know everywhere.
My first few months here were busy. Now that I’ve been here a while, I’ve started making friends, getting into a rhythm living on my own and establishing a professional reputation. I’m thankful for that. I’m also incredibly thankful to my amazing girlfriend, Lilly, who has afforded me so much leeway the last four years, such as when I run out to cover breaking news late in the evening or go out on the town to take photos for our morning weather posts.
Last weekend,
Cheyenne celebrated the start of the holiday season with a couple of really exciting downtown events
. Seeing so many people gather in the Depot Plaza and hearing them all count down the lighting of the Christmas tree, I was charmed. I felt something inside that was so close to clicking into place finally come together. Cheyenne had gone from a city I lived and worked in to my home. I’m thankful for that.
Thank you, readers, for supporting us as we continue to gain footing in this media market. Thank you for coming to us with ideas and corrections. Thank you for appreciating the free, local-first news we work so hard to put to screen. You are why we do what we do.
Please have a happy Thanksgiving. If you are traveling like me, please get to your destination safely. I plan on going back to Casper to see my family, cheer on my Chicago Bears as they face the dastardly Detroit Lions and stuff my face with my mom’s delicious cooking. See you again soon!
