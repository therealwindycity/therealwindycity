‘Girls on the Run’ marks 10th year bringing physical and emotional health to Wyoming youth
CHEYENNE, Wyo. –– Research has shown for years that good physical health often leads to better emotional health. That knowledge is what drove Cheyenne native Cindy Bradley a decade ago to bring a program promoting both to Wyoming youth.
“I would go down [to Denver] and volunteer for Girls on the Run of the Rockies, and was so impressed with the connectedness and self-confidence-building and the positive atmosphere,” she said. “I wanted to see if it was in Wyoming, and it was not.”
The Girls on the Run program was formed 30 years ago for elementary school–age girls in grades 3–6, and has since expanded through the U.S. and parts of Canada. Some chapters offer the program up to the junior high level, as high as eighth grade.
According to its website
, the program uses volunteer coaches to blend life skills and running, helping to manage emotions, increase empathy and build friendships.
“I had two young girls at the time and thought it was such an influential age and that girls need this,” Cindy said. “I looked into it and Wyoming was the only state that did not offer the program, and that was crazy to me.”
She started the nonprofit Wyoming chapter and was able to recruit 32 girls in their first season. This year, they had more than 700 participate, she said.
According to Cindy, Girls on the Run is an eight-week after-school program starting at the start of the fall school year. Attendees meet twice weekly with coaches and participate in running practice mixed with life lessons. At the end of the session, they celebrate with a 5K that is open to the public. Not only is the public encouraged to watch, but people are also encouraged to run in the 5K, according to Girls on the Run Wyoming’s program director, Kate Wright.
“In 2025, Gillette’s 5K only had 33 community runners,” Kate said. “This year they had 106.” The numbers in the recent Cheyenne race also saw a bump, going from 155 last year to nearly 200 this year.
Participants do activities before the Girls on the Run Wyoming 5K recently in Cheyenne. (Photo courtesy Karla Walden Photography via Girls on the Run Wyoming)
Running the celebratory season-ending 5K is where the students get to show their skills and enjoy the friendships they built in front of the community. “It’s a tangible sense of accomplishment for these girls crossing the finish line,” Cindy said. “It just instills that confidence through completion, and it’s a great way for the community to see who’s graduating the program.”
As far as activities go, running is one of the most basic and cost-effective. Instruct a student on form and training, and all they need is some open track and a pair of shoes. “It’s inexpensive; you don’t have to fight for court time like other sports or clubs, you can do it indoors or outdoors, so we just hope that it’s something that can sustain them year after year,” Cindy said.
“We don’t care if they walk, skip, jump or run,” she continued. “This is a non-competitive program. It’s about finishing what you start, and it’s about setting goals.”
The change Cindy and Kate see in the students is palpable. “Teachers tell us that the girls in the program are raising their hands more in class, making eye contact with adults, and using their voice to advocate,” Cindy said.
They learn to deal with anxiety using breathing techniques, relying less on their electronic devices and screens for escape. “It’s such an influential time in their lives, and we just want to keep eyes on them, give them something else for healthy choices and a sense of accomplishment,” she added.
Cindy recalled one student in particular who was transformed during her time in the program. She had a mohawk dyed purple, but was painfully shy and isolated. On the day of the 5K, she went back after finishing to cheer and run with the other students. “Afterwards, she had just had her [bat mitzvah] and got money from family, and she came and donated it back to the program and said it was life-changing,” Cindy said.
They understand that a nice pair of running shoes could also be out of reach for many students. “Adidas is one of our partners and national sponsors, so they give us discounts for those who need shoes,” she said.
The Wyoming program currently has chapters in five counties: Laramie, Goshen, Campbell, Sheridan and Sweetwater. They hope to expand, with Natrona County at the top on the priority list, along with Fremont and Park counties.
“I would say those are our obvious top tier, but we would love to be in every county across Wyoming,” Kate said. “We just need to see interest in the school district to get our foot in the door.”
