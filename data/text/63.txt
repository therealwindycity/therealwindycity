CASPER, Wyo. — A Laramie man has been sentenced to 30 years in federal prison for sexual exploitation of a minor and child sexual abuse material offenses.
Officials say that while Michael Collins Guenzel was 23 years old and serving felony probation in Albany County, he posed online as a 16-year-old boy to target and groom a 13-year-old victim, according to a Justice Department release.
Now 24 years old, Guenzel was sentenced by U.S. District Court Judge Alan B. Johnson on eight federal felony counts.  Guenzel pleaded guilty in June to five counts of sexual exploitation of a minor, one count of travel with intent to engage in illicit sexual conduct with a minor, one count of receipt of child sexual abuse material and one count of possession of child sexual abuse material.
“Continuing to misrepresent his age and identity, Guenzel met the child at a hotel and sexually abused her,” the release said. “The crimes came to light after Guenzel’s state probation officer searched his phone and discovered sexually explicit files depicting a young girl.”
Upon learning of the subsequent law enforcement investigation, Guenzel texted associates and instructed them not to speak with police, officials said. The probation agent contacted the Laramie Police Department, which handed the case over to the Wyoming Division of Criminal Investigation’s Internet Crimes Against Children Task Force.
“Justice cannot erase the lasting harm done to this poor girl, but today’s sentence ensures this predator is held accountable,” said U.S. Attorney Darin Smith.
On July 10, 2026, Guenzel pleaded guilty to eight federal felony counts: five counts of sexual exploitation of a minor, one count of travel with intent to engage in illicit sexual conduct with a minor, one count of receipt of child sexual abuse material and one count of possession of child sexual abuse material.
“This outcome is a direct result of teamwork across our criminal justice system, from the vigilance of state probation and the partnership of the Laramie Police Department to the dedicated investigators of our Internet Crimes Against Children Task Force and the prosecutor at the U.S. Attorney’s Office,” said Ryan Cox, commander of the Wyoming Division of Criminal Investigation.
