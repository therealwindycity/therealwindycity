Chilly, breezy weekend in Cheyenne before midweek warmup
CHEYENNE, Wyo. — Cheyenne and Laramie County residents can expect a chilly, breezy weekend with little chance of measurable snowfall before temperatures warm back into the upper 40s and low 50s ahead of Thanksgiving, according to the National Weather Service in Cheyenne.
While radar returns showed light activity near Cheyenne eastward into the southern Nebraska Panhandle early Saturday morning, dry air near the surface kept most precipitation from reaching the ground, meteorologists said in a 3:55 a.m. forecast discussion. Precipitation chances around the capital city remained below 15% Saturday morning, with breezy westerly winds set to persist through the afternoon.
Heavier snow shower activity Saturday afternoon and early evening is expected to remain well north and west of Laramie County, focusing primarily on Carbon County and areas along and north of the U.S. Highway 20 corridor, including Converse County. Forecasters noted those areas could see quick-hitting snow showers or localized snow squalls capable of dropping a dusting to 2 inches of snow and rapidly reducing visibility for travelers.
Once the upper-level system clears the region Saturday evening, surface high pressure will settle over southeast Wyoming, ushering in a cold Sunday morning with the possibility of shallow low clouds or fog across the plains. Daytime highs on Sunday will remain chilly across Laramie County and the surrounding region, reaching only the upper 30s to lower 40s.
Cool conditions will linger through the start of the workweek as increasing cloud cover and an entrenched surface high keep temperatures suppressed on Monday. While a weak Pacific system is expected to bring light snow to the mountains Monday night into Tuesday morning, lower elevations around Cheyenne are expected to stay largely dry. Sunshine will return Tuesday with chilly daytime highs and breezy westerly winds picking up by night, when the probability of high winds rises to between 30% and 40%.
A high-pressure ridge is forecast to rebuild over the region Wednesday, kicking off a warming trend that will push afternoon highs into the mid-40s to low 50s through the end of the week.
Forecasters noted significant uncertainty heading into Thanksgiving Day on Thursday, as weather models remain split on the track of the next system. One primary forecast model keeps the storm track far to the south, resulting in dry, warm weather and lighter winds for southeast Wyoming through Friday. Another major model keeps the system closer to the state, which would bring a chance of light snow showers Wednesday night, increased mountain snow and a heightened potential for high winds across southeast Wyoming on Thursday and Friday.
